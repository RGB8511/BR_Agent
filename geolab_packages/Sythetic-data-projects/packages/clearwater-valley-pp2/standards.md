**Report Basis:** WGC Report 2018-GEO-0093; CVID 2024 Annual Facility Assessment; CVID Preliminary Design Memo TK-2A July 2025

This document catalogs all standards, specifications, and codes cited in or applicable to the PP-2 geotechnical investigation, foundation design, construction verification, and proposed TK-2A surge tank design. ## Sampling and In-Situ Testing

### ASTM D1586 — Standard Test Method for Standard Penetration Test (SPT) and Split-Barrel Sampling of Soils

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM D1586-18a
**Scope:** Prescribes the procedure for advancing a split-barrel sampler into soil at the bottom of a borehole using blows from a 140-lb hammer dropped 30 inches. Records the number of blows required for each 6-inch increment of penetration; N-value = sum of blows for the second and third increments (blows per foot). Provides a disturbed soil sample for identification and index testing.

**PP-2 Application:** All SPT tests in BH-PP2-01 through BH-PP2-04 were performed per ASTM D1586. An automatic hammer was used with energy calibration confirming energy ratio (ER) of 97–105%, yielding a correction factor CE = ER/60 = 0.97–1.05 applied to raw N-values to obtain N₆₀. A total of 52 SPT tests were performed across all borings. SPT N-values were used for: (1) soil classification and stratigraphic correlation; (2) relative density estimation for liquefaction analysis; (3) bearing capacity and settlement parameters for MCC spread footings (Bowles N-based E correlation).

**Key clauses:**
- Section 5 (Apparatus): Split-barrel sampler dimensions, hammer type specifications
- Section 7 (Procedure): Drive sequence, blow count recording, sample recovery
- Section 8 (Calculation and Report): N-value definition; energy correction requirements

### ASTM D1587 — Standard Practice for Thin-Walled Tube Sampling of Fine-Grained Soils for Geotechnical Purposes

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM D1587-15
**Scope:** Describes the procedure for obtaining relatively undisturbed soil samples from cohesive fine-grained soils using thin-walled (Shelby) tube samplers. Sampler is pushed (not driven) into soil using continuous hydraulic pressure at a rate of approximately 2 cm/s. Sample is sealed with wax and end caps immediately after recovery to preserve in-situ moisture content and structure for laboratory testing.

**PP-2 Application:** Six (6) Shelby tube samples (ST-1 through ST-6) were collected from Layer 2 alluvial clay in BH-PP2-01 through BH-PP2-04. Tubes were 3-inch (76.2 mm) outer diameter. Samples were used for: (1) moisture content determination; (2) Atterberg limit testing (ASTM D4318); (3) one-dimensional swell/consolidation testing (ASTM D4546/D2435). Sample quality was assessed using the Lunne et al. (1997) criteria based on Δe/e₀; all ST samples classified as Good to Excellent quality.

**Key clauses:**
- Section 5 (Apparatus): Tube dimensions, area ratio, inside clearance ratio
- Section 7 (Procedure): Tube advancement rate, hydraulic push requirements
- Section 8 (Sample Preservation): Sealing, labeling, transport temperature

### ASTM D6032 — Standard Test Method for Determining Rock Quality Designation (RQD) of Rock Core

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM D6032-17
**Scope:** Defines RQD as the cumulative length of naturally intact core pieces ≥ 100 mm (4 inches) in length, expressed as a percentage of the total length of a core run. RQD is a fundamental index for describing rock mass discontinuity, used in RMR (Bieniawski 1989) and Q-system (Barton et al. 1974) rock mass classification, and in FHWA guidance for drilled shaft socket friction reduction.

**PP-2 Application:** RQD was measured on 7 core runs from BH-PP2-03 (3 runs) and BH-PP2-04 (4 runs). Core box photographs were archived. RQD values ranged from 45% (highly weathered top-of-rock) to 78% (fresh ignimbrite at depth). Rock quality classes per Deere & Deere (1988): 45–50% = Fair; 64–78% = Good. Design RQD = 45% (lower bound) used in conservative rock mass classification for socket friction computation.

**Key clauses:**
- Section 3 (Terminology): RQD definition and measurement criteria
- Section 5 (Procedure): Core run length measurement, piece length determination
- Section 6 (Reporting): Tabular reporting requirements, piece count

## Index and Classification Testing

### ASTM D2216 — Standard Test Methods for Laboratory Determination of Water (Moisture) Content of Soil and Rock by Mass

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM D2216-19
**Scope:** Determines the water content of soil and rock specimens as the ratio of mass of water to mass of dry solids. Three methods: Method A (total evaporable moisture, 110°C oven); Method B (total moisture including adsorbed water); Method C (field-deployable microwave method). ASTM D2216 Method A is the standard reference method for geotechnical work.

**PP-2 Application:** Thirteen (13) moisture content tests performed on split-spoon and Shelby tube trimmings from all stratigraphic layers. Results used for: (1) soil classification (comparison with Atterberg limits); (2) initial void ratio for consolidation specimens; (3) compaction QC baseline. All tests performed by Method A (110°C oven, ≥ 16 hours drying time, to constant mass).

**Key clauses:**
- Section 7 (Equipment): Oven temperature tolerance ± 5°C
- Section 8 (Procedure): Sample size requirements, drying criteria
- Section 9 (Calculations): w = (Mw/Ms) × 100%

### ASTM D2487 — Standard Practice for Classification of Soils for Engineering Purposes (Unified Soil Classification System)

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM D2487-17e1
**Scope:** Defines the Unified Soil Classification System (USCS) for classifying soils into groups with similar engineering properties. Classification is based on particle size distribution, liquid limit, and plasticity index. Produces 2-letter symbols (e.g., CL, SM, GW) corresponding to major soil types (C = clay, M = silt, S = sand, G = gravel) and soil characteristics (L = low plasticity, H = high plasticity, W = well-graded, P = poorly-graded).

**PP-2 Application:** USCS classification applied to all soil samples from the four borings. Layer 2 uniformly classified as CL (Lean Clay with Sand); Layer 3 as SM (Silty Sand) to ML (Sandy Silt); Layer 1 as SM to GM. Classifications confirmed by laboratory Atterberg limits and sieve analysis. USCS symbols are referenced throughout the boring logs and geotechnical report.

**Key clauses:**
- Section 4 (Classification Procedure): Flowchart for classification by particle size and plasticity
- Table 1: Classification criteria for coarse-grained soils
- Table 2: Classification criteria for fine-grained soils (Casagrande chart basis)

### ASTM D4318 — Standard Test Methods for Liquid Limit, Plastic Limit, and Plasticity Index of Soils

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM D4318-17e1
**Scope:** Determines the Atterberg limits of fine-grained soils. Three methods for liquid limit: Method A (multi-point Casagrande cup), Method B (single-point Casagrande), Method C (fall-cone). Plastic limit determined by rolled-thread method. Plasticity index PI = LL – PL.

**PP-2 Application:** Seven (7) Atterberg limit tests on Layer 2 alluvial clay. WGC laboratory used the multi-point fall-cone method (Method C, 4-point LL) for improved precision. Results: LL = 35–41, PL = 18–21, PI = 17–20. All samples plot above A-line on Casagrande chart confirming CL classification. PI and activity values used to assess expansive potential and corroborate WRI swell potential classification.

**Key clauses:**
- Section 6 (Liquid Limit by Casagrande Method): Cup dimensions, blow count procedure
- Section 7 (Plastic Limit): Rolling thread diameter, thread failure criteria
- Section 10 (Calculations): LL from flow curve; PI = LL – PL; Activity = PI / clay fraction

### ASTM D6913 — Standard Test Methods for Particle-Size Distribution (Gradation) of Soils Using Sieve Analysis

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM D6913-17
**Scope:** Determines the particle-size distribution (gradation) of coarse-grained soils by passing oven-dried disaggregated soil through a series of standard sieves. Covers fractions from 3-inch (75 mm) to No. 200 (75-μm) sieve sizes. Results plotted as gradation curve (percent finer vs. grain size).

**PP-2 Application:** Sieve analysis performed on six (6) samples from Layers 1 and 3 to confirm USCS classification and determine fines content for liquefaction analysis. Layer 3 SM samples showed fines content (passing No. 200) of 18–32%, consistent with SM classification. Fines content used in Youd et al. (2001) fines correction factor for liquefaction resistance.

**Key clauses:**
- Section 5 (Apparatus): Standard sieve sizes, mechanical shaker specifications
- Section 7 (Procedure): Washing procedure for fines determination (combined with D7928)
- Section 8 (Calculations): Percent passing each sieve size; D10, D30, D50, D60 computations

### ASTM D7928 — Standard Test Method for Particle-Size Distribution (Gradation) of Fine-Grained Soils Using the Sedimentation (Hydrometer) Analysis

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM D7928-21
**Scope:** Determines the particle-size distribution of fine-grained fraction (< No. 200 sieve) using the sedimentation method based on Stokes' law. Measures hydrometer density readings at specified time intervals to compute percentage of particles finer than various equivalent diameters. Required for accurate clay fraction determination needed for Activity calculation and expansive soil classification.

**PP-2 Application:** Hydrometer analysis performed in combination with sieve analysis on three (3) Layer 2 clay samples. Clay fraction (< 2 μm) measured as 17.9–23.1% by weight for computation of Activity = PI / clay fraction. Clay fraction also used in swell potential assessment.

**Key clauses:**
- Section 6 (Apparatus): Hydrometer type (ASTM 152H), cylinder dimensions
- Section 7 (Dispersing Agent): Sodium hexametaphosphate concentration and preparation
- Section 10 (Calculations): Stokes' law diameter, time-temperature corrections

### ASTM D698 — Standard Test Methods for Laboratory Compaction Characteristics of Soil Using Standard Effort (12,400 ft-lbf/ft³)

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM D698-12(2021)
**Scope:** Determines the relationship between moisture content and dry density (compaction curve) for soils compacted using a 5.5-lb hammer dropped 12 inches in a standard 4-inch or 6-inch mold. Provides maximum dry density (MDD) and optimum moisture content (OMC) at standard Proctor energy level (corresponds to typical light construction compaction). Minimum 5 data points required to define curve peak.

**PP-2 Application:** One (1) standard Proctor test on structural fill material (blended Layer 2 clay + clean pit-run sand to simulate borrow). Results: MDD = 115.2 pcf, OMC = 13.8%. These values established the compaction specification for structural fill (≥ 95% of MDD at OMC ± 2%) used for over-excavation backfill, wall backfill, and vault backfill. Construction compaction results: 96.2–98.8% of MDD (all tests passing).

**Key clauses:**
- Section 6 (Apparatus): Hammer, mold, and extension collar specifications
- Section 8 (Procedure): Number of layers, blows per layer, mold size selection based on maximum particle size
- Section 9 (Calculations): Dry density from wet density and moisture content; compaction curve plotting

## Strength Testing

### ASTM D7012 — Standard Test Methods for Compressive Strength and Elastic Moduli of Intact Rock Core Specimens under Varying States of Stress and Temperatures

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM D7012-14e1
**Scope:** Covers four methods for determining strength of intact rock core: Method A (triaxial compressive strength and elastic moduli, confining pressure), Method B (uniaxial compressive strength only), Method C (Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio in addition to UCS), and Method D (Brazilian tensile strength — cross-referenced to D3967). Method C is the most commonly specified for design-level investigations as it provides both strength and stiffness simultaneously.

**PP-2 Application:** Twelve (12) UCS tests performed by Method C (UCS + E50 + ν) on NX core from BH-PP2-03 and BH-PP2-04. Specimens trimmed to L/D = 2.07–2.11. Axial and lateral strain gauges bonded to specimen mid-height. Load applied at constant displacement rate 0.5 mm/min. E50 and Poisson's ratio computed from stress-strain plots at 50% of peak stress. Results used for drilled shaft end-bearing and socket friction design.

**Key clauses:**
- Section 5 (Specimen Preparation): End flatness tolerance ≤ 0.025 mm; perpendicularity ± 0.25°
- Section 7C (Procedure — Method C): Strain gauge attachment, data acquisition rate
- Section 9 (Calculations): Axial stress, axial strain, volumetric strain, E50, ν at 50% peak load
- Section 10 (Report): Required reporting items including L/D ratio, moisture condition, failure mode

### ASTM D3967 — Standard Test Method for Splitting Tensile Strength of Intact Rock Core Specimens

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM D3967-16
**Scope:** Determines the splitting tensile strength of rock (Brazilian test) by diametrically loading a disc specimen to failure. Loading is applied through curved bearing platens or flat platens with load-distributing strips. Indirect tensile strength (ITS) = 2P / (π × D × t), where P is peak load, D is disc diameter, and t is disc thickness. Provides a simple, reproducible indirect measure of tensile strength for use in rock mass classification and correlation with UCS (UCS/ITS ratio).

**PP-2 Application:** Fourteen (14) ITS tests on NX discs from 7 core runs. Disc L/D ≈ 0.50 (D = 54.7 mm, t = 27.1–27.5 mm). Load applied at 0.2 mm/min. ITS results 540–890 psi (mean 683 psi). UCS/ITS ratio 9.7. ITS values used in Hoek-Brown rock mass strength analysis for rock mass classification and as indirect check on UCS data reliability.

**Key clauses:**
- Section 5 (Apparatus): Loading platens, bearing strip material and dimensions
- Section 7 (Procedure): Specimen orientation marking, loading rate
- Section 8 (Calculations): ITS = 2P/(πDt); failure load reading

## Consolidation and Swell Testing

### ASTM D4546 — Standard Test Methods for One-Dimensional Swell or Collapse of Soils

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM D4546-21
**Scope:** Determines one-dimensional swell or collapse potential of cohesive soils upon wetting. Three methods: Method A (measure swell under initial stress, then reduce stress to zero to measure total swell); Method B (constant volume — apply load to maintain zero vertical strain during flooding, then unload to free-swell curve); Method C (swell under nominal seating load only). Method B provides both swell pressure (stress at constant volume) and subsequent free-swell behavior on the same specimen.

**PP-2 Application:** Four (4) swell/consolidation tests by Method B on Layer 2 alluvial clay specimens. Initial loading to in-situ overburden stress before flooding. Swell pressure measured at constant volume state (1,050–1,620 psf). Specimens then unloaded and reloaded to define full swell/consolidation curve. Free swell at in-situ stress ranged 2.8–4.1%. Results classified the clay as moderately expansive per WRI (1965) chart. Swell pressure used to design 4-inch void forms beneath grade beams.

**Key clauses:**
- Section 6 (Apparatus): Consolidometer ring dimensions, loading frame
- Section 7B (Method B Procedure): Load application to in-situ stress, flooding sequence, constant volume adjustment
- Section 8 (Calculations): Free swell percentage; swell pressure determination at zero strain

### ASTM D4972 — Standard Test Methods for pH of Soils

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM D4972-19
**Scope:** Determines the pH of soil using either a glass electrode pH meter (Method A) or colorimetric indicator paper (Method B). Method A (pH meter) is required for design-level corrosion assessments. Soil suspension prepared at 1:1 or 1:5 soil-to-water ratio in deionized water; pH measured after 30-minute equilibration.

**PP-2 Application:** Three (3) soil pH tests by Method A on Layer 2 alluvial clay corrosion samples. pH = 7.2–8.1, indicating slightly alkaline conditions. Alkaline pH moderately protective for embedded steel (passive oxide film formation). Combined with resistivity results, pH data confirmed moderately corrosive classification per NACE RP-0169.

**Key clauses:**
- Section 6 (Equipment): Electrode type, calibration buffer requirements
- Section 7 (Procedure): Sample preparation, equilibration time, reading sequence
- Section 8 (Report): pH value to nearest 0.1 unit

## Corrosion Testing

### ASTM G57 — Standard Test Method for Field Measurement of Soil Resistivity Using the Wenner Four-Electrode Method

**Issuing body:** ASTM International
**Current edition referenced:** ASTM G57-06(2012)
**Scope:** Measures apparent soil electrical resistivity in the field using four electrodes driven into the soil at equal spacing a. For uniform soil, the apparent resistivity ρ = 2πaR, where R is the measured resistance (Ω). Electrode spacing a corresponds approximately to the depth of measurement. Provides a field-screening tool for corrosion assessment of buried metallic structures; low resistivity indicates high ionic concentration and high corrosion risk.

**PP-2 Application:** Three (3) soil resistivity tests performed on disturbed soil samples reconstituted to field moisture content in accordance with ASTM G57 modified box procedure (laboratory equivalent of field Wenner method). Resistivity values 3,800–5,100 Ω·cm. NACE classification: Moderately Corrosive (2,000–10,000 Ω·cm). Results drove the specification for Type II cement, epoxy-coated rebar, and cathodic protection for buried pipelines.

**Key clauses:**
- Section 4 (Apparatus): Electrode spacing requirements, resistance meter specifications
- Section 5 (Procedure): Electrode driving procedure, multiple reading directions
- Section 6 (Calculations): Resistivity from spacing and measured resistance

## Structural Design Standards

### ASCE 7-16 — Minimum Design Loads and Associated Criteria for Buildings and Other Structures

**Issuing body:** American Society of Civil Engineers / Structural Engineering Institute
**Edition used:** ASCE 7-16 (2016)
**Scope:** Provides minimum design loads (dead, live, wind, snow, rain, ice, flood, earthquake) and load combinations for buildings and other structures. Chapter 11 and 12 cover seismic design requirements. Chapter 20 defines site class procedure and site amplification factors Fa and Fv. Chapter 11.8 covers geotechnical investigation requirements for seismic design categories C through F.

**PP-2 Application:** ASCE 7-16 was the governing structural design code for PP-2. Specific applications:
- **Section 11.4:** Ground motion parameters (Ss = 0.35g, S1 = 0.13g, PGA = 0.15g) from USGS online hazard tool.
- **Section 11.8.3:** Geotechnical investigation scope requirements for Seismic Design Category C (Site Class D).
- **Table 11.4-1:** Site coefficient Fa = 1.58 (Site Class D, Ss = 0.35g).
- **Table 11.4-2:** Site coefficient Fv = 2.40 (Site Class D, S1 = 0.13g).
- **Section 20.3:** Site Class D classification based on average Vs30 = 500–900 ft/s for alluvial sand profile.
- **Chapter 12 (Seismic Design Requirements):** Lateral force design basis for pump bay structure.
- **Section 11.8:** Lateral earth pressure increase for seismic design (25% increase on at-rest pressures for out-of-plane loads on pump bay walls).

**Key clauses:**
- Chapter 11: Seismic hazard parameters and site coefficients
- Chapter 12: Equivalent lateral force procedure
- Chapter 20: Site classification
- Section 11.8: Geotechnical investigation requirements

### ACI 318-19 — Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete

**Issuing body:** American Concrete Institute
**Edition used:** ACI 318-19 (with commentary ACI 318R-19)
**Scope:** Governs the design and construction of structural concrete buildings and structures in the United States. Chapter 19 addresses concrete durability requirements including exposure class designations for sulfate attack, chloride corrosion, and freeze-thaw. Chapter 26 covers construction document requirements for concrete mix design and placement.

**PP-2 Application:** ACI 318-19 governed the structural concrete design for the pump bay, MCC building, and discharge vault. Geotechnical-relevant provisions:
- **Table 19.3.3.1 (Sulfate Exposure):** Water-soluble sulfate 280–420 ppm → Exposure Class S1 → Required cement: Type II (MS) or equivalent SCM blend. Minimum w/cm ≤ 0.50 for S1 (WGC specified more restrictive ≤ 0.45 for buried elements).
- **Section 20.6.1 (Concrete Cover for Corrosion Protection):** Cover requirements for reinforcing steel based on exposure condition. WGC specified 3-inch cover for buried elements (exceeds ACI 318 minimum of 2 inches for concrete not exposed to earth).
- **Table 19.3.1 (Freeze-Thaw Exposure):** Exposure Class F0 for below-grade elements (not critically saturated); Exposure Class F1 for elements exposed to freezing while wet.
- **Section 26.4.2.1:** Maximum w/cm for water-soluble chloride limits to avoid corrosion of embedded steel.

**Key clauses:**
- Chapter 19: Concrete durability requirements (sulfate, chloride, F-T exposure classes)
- Chapter 20: Steel reinforcement — cover requirements
- Chapter 26: Construction document requirements for concrete mix design

## Seismic Standards

*(ASCE 7-16 is the primary seismic standard and is documented in the Structural Design section above. The following supplements are referenced for liquefaction assessment methodology.)*

**Youd et al. (2001) — Liquefaction Resistance of Soils: Summary Report from the 1996 NCEER and 1998 NCEER/NSF Workshops on Evaluation of Liquefaction Resistance of Soils**

**Published in:** ASCE Journal of Geotechnical and Geoenvironmental Engineering, Vol. 127, No. 10, pp. 817–833.
**Scope:** Provides the consensus-based SPT simplified procedure for evaluation of liquefaction triggering resistance. Includes updated CRR-N₁(60)cs boundary curves, energy correction procedure, fines content corrections, magnitude scaling factors (MSF), and overburden correction factors.

**PP-2 Application:** Used as the primary method for liquefaction evaluation at PP-2. SPT N-values corrected to N₁(60)cs; CSR computed for PGA = 0.15g, Mw = 6.5; MSF = 1.32 applied; FS_liq = CRR × MSF / CSR. All computed FS_liq > 2.24 at PP-2 (minimum value at shallowest water-table depth). No liquefaction mitigation required.

---

**Robertson & Wride (1998) — Evaluating Cyclic Liquefaction Potential Using the Cone Penetration Test**

**Published in:** Canadian Geotechnical Journal, Vol. 35, No. 3, pp. 442–459.
**Scope:** Provides fines correction for SPT N-values in liquefaction analysis when fines content FC > 5%. Correction equation: Δ(N₁)₆₀ = exp(1.63 + 9.7/(FC + 0.01) – (15.7/(FC + 0.01))²).

**PP-2 Application:** Layer 3 SM materials with FC = 18–32% required fines correction before CRR determination. Corrections increased effective N₁(60)cs by 4–8 blows/ft, improving computed liquefaction resistance above the clean-sand reference value.

## Water Storage Tank Design

### AWWA D100 — Welded Carbon Steel Tanks for Water Storage

**Issuing body:** American Water Works Association
**Current edition referenced:** AWWA D100-11 (reaffirmed 2016)
**Scope:** Comprehensive standard for the design, fabrication, and construction of welded carbon steel tanks for water storage, including elevated tanks, standpipes, and ground-level storage tanks. Covers structural design, material specifications, welding requirements, coatings, and foundation design criteria. Section 14 specifically addresses foundation design for welded steel tanks, including ringwall, slab-on-grade, and pile foundations.

**TK-2A Application:** AWWA D100 is the governing design standard for the proposed TK-2A surge tank at PP-2. Specific provisions applicable to the geotechnical scope:

- **Section 3 (General Design Criteria):** Shell height-to-diameter ratio limits; freeboard requirements for surge service.
- **Section 4.4 (Anchorage):** Anchor bolt design procedure for overturning resistance under empty tank condition with wind and seismic loading. Requires FS ≥ 1.5 against overturning.
- **Table 2-1 (Steel Materials):** ASTM A36 for shell plates ≤ 0.5 inch thickness; A516 Grade 70 for heavier plates.
- **Section 9 (Coatings):** Internal coating per AWWA C210 (liquid epoxy); external coating per SSPC standards.
- **Section 14 (Foundation Design):** Ringwall foundation requirements; allowable bearing pressure verification; settlement tolerance = tilt ≤ 1 in per 200 ft (1/200) of diameter, which for a 40-ft diameter tank ≈ 0.02 ft = 0.24 in (approximately ≈ 1/500 of diameter equivalently stated elsewhere; design uses 0.6 in max differential).
- **Appendix A (Seismic Design):** Ground-supported tank seismic design procedure per ASCE 7-16 Chapter 15 and API 650 Appendix E; convective and impulsive mass components.

**Key clauses:**
- Section 4.4: Anchor bolt design — overturning check
- Section 14: Foundation types and bearing pressure requirements
- Appendix A: Seismic design for ground-supported tanks
- Section 3: Allowable stresses and load combinations
