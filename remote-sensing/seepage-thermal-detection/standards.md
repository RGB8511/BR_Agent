# Standards Reference — Seepage & Thermal Anomaly Detection

## FEMA P-1025 — Federal Guidelines for Dam Safety: Monitoring of Dams
**Scope:** FEMA guidelines for dam monitoring including seepage monitoring methods. Covers visual observation, weir measurements, piezometry, and emerging technologies including thermal imagery and distributed temperature sensing. Framework for integrating thermal surveys into dam safety monitoring programs.

## USACE EM 1110-2-1901 — Seepage Analysis and Control for Dams
**Scope:** USACE manual for seepage analysis through and under dams. Covers seepage theory, flow nets, instrumentation, and remedial measures. Provides context for interpreting thermal seepage detections — correlating thermal anomalies with flow path analysis and piezometric data.

## USACE ETL 1110-2-586 — Design of Seepage and Drainage Features for Dams and Levees
**Scope:** Engineering technical letter on seepage control features. Filter design, drain design, relief wells, and cutoff walls. Thermal detection helps verify performance of these features.

## ASTM D4788 — Standard Test Method for Detecting Delaminations Using Infrared Thermography
**Scope:** While written for bridge deck delamination detection, the thermal survey methodology (weather conditions, camera requirements, data collection procedures) is directly applicable to seepage thermal surveys on concrete structures. Referenced as the closest ASTM standard for thermal IR field surveys.

## ISO 18434-1 — Condition Monitoring and Diagnostics of Machines — Thermography — Part 1: General Procedures
**Scope:** ISO standard for infrared thermography procedures. Covers camera calibration, environmental corrections (emissivity, reflected temperature, atmospheric transmission), and reporting requirements. Applicable to quantitative thermal surveys for seepage detection.

## Henault et al. (2010) — Quantitative Strain and Temperature Monitoring Using Distributed Fiber Optic Sensing
**Scope:** Key reference for fiber-optic distributed temperature sensing (DTS) applied to infrastructure monitoring. Covers Raman and Brillouin scattering principles, spatial resolution, temperature accuracy, and installation methods for dam and levee monitoring.

## Wells & Lovelace (2018) — Unmanned Aircraft System (UAS) Applications to Dam Safety
**Scope:** USBR/FEMA guidance on applying UAS technology to dam safety inspections including thermal seepage surveys. Flight planning, sensor selection, data processing, and integration with dam safety programs. Practical guidance specific to dam/levee thermal surveys.
