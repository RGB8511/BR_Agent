# Protective Coating Systems

## Purpose and Philosophy

Protective coatings isolate the metal substrate from the corrosive environment. A coating system is a multi-layer assembly designed for specific service conditions:

1. **Primer:** Adheres to substrate, provides corrosion inhibition (barrier, sacrificial, or inhibitive)
2. **Intermediate/build coat:** Adds thickness (barrier), enhances chemical resistance
3. **Topcoat/finish:** UV resistance, color, abrasion resistance, aesthetics

## Coating Types by Chemistry

### Epoxy Coatings
Thermoset — two-component (resin + hardener). Excellent adhesion, chemical resistance, and barrier properties. The workhorse of industrial coatings.

- **Amine-cured epoxy:** Most common. Good chemical resistance. Tends to chalk under UV.
- **Polyamide epoxy:** Better flexibility and moisture resistance than amine-cured. Slightly less chemical resistance.
- **Phenolic epoxy (novolac):** Superior chemical/solvent resistance. Used for tank linings, immersion service.
- **Coal tar epoxy:** Excellent water/moisture resistance. Declining use due to health concerns.

**Limitation:** Epoxies chalk and degrade under UV exposure — always topcoat for exterior service.

### Zinc-Rich Primers
Provide sacrificial (galvanic) protection. Zinc corrodes preferentially, protecting underlying steel even at holidays and damage.

- **Inorganic zinc (IOZ):** Ethyl silicate or alkali silicate binder with >75% zinc dust by weight in dry film. Extremely durable, heat resistant. Requires careful surface preparation (SSPC-SP 10 or SP 5). Industry gold standard for long-term protection.
- **Organic zinc:** Epoxy binder with high zinc loading. Easier to apply, more tolerant of surface prep. Less sacrificial protection than IOZ.

### Polyurethane Coatings
Two-component — polyol + isocyanate. Excellent UV resistance, gloss retention, color stability, abrasion resistance. The standard topcoat for exterior exposure.

- **Aliphatic polyurethane:** Superior UV resistance (exterior topcoat standard)
- **Aromatic polyurethane:** Cheaper, but yellows rapidly in sunlight. Interior only.
- **Polyurea/polyaspartic:** Very fast cure, thick film capability. Bridge decks, secondary containment.

### Alkyd and Oil-Based Coatings
Single-component, air-drying. Economical for mild atmospheric exposure. Limited chemical resistance. Being phased out by low-VOC regulations in many regions.

### Fluoropolymer Coatings
FEVE and PVDF chemistries. Exceptional UV resistance, color retention, chemical resistance. 20–30 year service life in atmospheric exposure. Premium cost.

### Thermal Spray (Metallizing)
Arc or flame spray of zinc, aluminum, or 85/15 Zn-Al onto abrasive blasted surface. Provides sacrificial protection + barrier. Extremely long life in severe environments (marine, immersion). Must be sealed with penetrating sealer.

## Film Thickness

### Wet Film Thickness (WFT)
Measured during application with a wet film gauge. Used to predict DFT:

DFT = WFT × (% volume solids / 100)

### Dry Film Thickness (DFT)
Measured after cure with magnetic or eddy current gauges (SSPC-PA 2). Critical quality control measurement.

**Spread rate:** Theoretical coverage (m²/L) = (% volume solids × 10) / target DFT (μm)
Practical coverage = theoretical × application efficiency (typically 50–80%)

### SSPC-PA 2 Measurement Protocol
Defines spot measurements, individual readings, and area averages. For each coat:
- Each spot measurement (average of 3 gauge readings) must exceed specified minimum
- Average of all spot readings must meet or exceed specified DFT
- No individual gauge reading below 80% of specified DFT (for ≤ 500 μm) or below specified minus 100 μm (for > 500 μm)

## Adhesion Testing

### Pull-Off Adhesion (ASTM D4541)
Dolly bonded to surface with adhesive, pulled perpendicular. Measures adhesion strength in MPa. Typical minimum: 3.5–5.0 MPa for industrial coatings. Record failure mode: adhesive (at interface), cohesive (within coating), or glue failure.

### Cross-Cut Adhesion (ASTM D3359)
Method A: X-cut with tape. Method B: crosshatch grid with tape. Rating 0A–5A or 0B–5B (5 = no removal, 0 = > 65% removed). Qualitative screening test.

## Holiday Detection

### Low Voltage (Wet Sponge)
67.5V wet sponge for thin films (< 500 μm). Detects pinholes, pores, thin spots.

### High Voltage (Spark Test)
Voltage calculated based on DFT. Detects holidays in thick coatings (> 500 μm). Per NACE SP0188:

V = 1250 × √DFT (μm) for brush type
V = 525 × √DFT (μm) for DC holiday detectors (some standards)

## Environmental Conditions During Application

- **Surface temperature:** ≥ 3°C (5°F) above dew point
- **Relative humidity:** Typically < 85% (product-specific)
- **Temperature range:** Product-specific; most epoxies 10–35°C, IOZ can be applied lower
- **Wind:** Excessive wind causes dry spray. Shelter may be required.

If conditions are outside specification → do not apply. This is the most common cause of coating failure.

## Service Life and Maintenance

Coating service life depends on system, environment, and surface preparation quality:
- **Mild atmospheric:** 15–25 years for IOZ + epoxy + PU system
- **Severe marine/industrial:** 10–20 years for same system
- **Immersion (freshwater):** 10–15 years for high-build epoxy/polyamide systems
- **Chemical immersion:** 5–10 years for novolac epoxy linings

Maintenance painting (spot repair) extends system life dramatically and is far more cost-effective than full recoating.
