# Canal Lining Design

## Purpose of Canal Lining

Canal lining serves multiple functions: reducing seepage losses, preventing erosion, enabling higher velocities (reducing canal cross-section), controlling weed growth, reducing maintenance, and preventing waterlogging and salinization of adjacent lands.

### Seepage Loss Reduction
Unlined canals in permeable soils can lose 20–50% of flow through seepage. Lining typically reduces seepage by 60–95% depending on lining type and condition. For arid-region irrigation projects, seepage control is often the primary economic justification for lining.

## Lining Types

### Hard Linings (Rigid)
**Unreinforced concrete:** Most common for large canals. Typical thickness 50–100 mm (2–4 in) for small canals, 75–150 mm (3–6 in) for large. Design life 40–60+ years. Placed by slip-form paving machine for large canals, hand-formed for small. Joints at 3–5 m spacing (transverse) with PVC waterstops or sealant.

**Reinforced concrete:** Used where differential settlement, frost heave, or hydrostatic uplift is expected. Welded wire fabric or rebar. Thickness 100–200 mm.

**Shotcrete (gunite):** Pneumatically applied concrete. Excellent for irregular shapes, rehabilitation of existing linings. 50–100 mm thickness. Good bond to substrate.

**Precast concrete panels:** Factory-made panels placed on prepared subgrade. Joints sealed with mortar or sealant. Used for standardized sections.

### Flexible Linings (Membrane)
**Geomembrane (HDPE, PVC, LLDPE, EPDM):** Thin synthetic membrane (0.5–2.0 mm) placed on prepared subgrade, typically covered with soil or concrete for protection. Very low permeability (essentially zero seepage). Requires careful seaming and protection from puncture and UV.

**Geosynthetic clay liner (GCL):** Bentonite clay sandwiched between geotextiles. Self-healing of small punctures. Good for irregular subgrades. Typically covered with soil.

**Compacted clay:** 150–300 mm of compacted clay (k < 10⁻⁷ cm/s). Low cost where clay is available locally. Susceptible to desiccation cracking and root penetration. Requires protective cover layer.

### Composite Linings
**Membrane + concrete:** Geomembrane as waterproofing layer under concrete wearing surface. Maximum seepage control with structural protection. Used for high-value water and critical conveyance.

**Soil-cement:** Mixed in-place soil with 6–12% cement. 100–150 mm compacted. Lower cost than concrete but less durable.

### Earth Linings
**Compacted earth:** Improved native soil or imported select fill. Thickness 300–600 mm. Moderate seepage reduction (50–80%). Lowest cost but requires more maintenance.

**Buried membrane:** Geomembrane placed within earthen lining, protected by soil above and below. Combines seepage control with natural channel appearance.

## Design Considerations

### Hydrostatic Uplift
When canal is empty or water level drops rapidly, external groundwater pressure can lift the lining (blowout). Design must include:
- Drainage behind lining (gravel blanket, perforated pipe drains, geotextile filters)
- Relief valves or weep holes through lining
- Weight of lining to resist uplift
- Operational procedures (maintain minimum water level, slow drawdown)

Critical condition: P_uplift = γ_w × h_gw > W_lining + W_water

### Frost Heave
In cold climates, frost penetration below the lining can heave and crack rigid linings. Mitigation:
- Drain the subgrade (reduce moisture available for ice lens formation)
- Increase lining thickness or add reinforcement
- Install granular insulation layer below lining
- Use flexible lining systems in severe frost areas

### Subgrade Preparation
Lining performance depends heavily on subgrade quality:
- Remove organic material, roots, large rocks
- Compact to ≥ 95% standard Proctor
- Grade to design cross-section ± 25 mm tolerance
- Moisture condition to near optimum
- For membranes: smooth surface free of puncture hazards

### Joints (Rigid Linings)
- Contraction joints: Formed or saw-cut at regular intervals (3–5 m) to control cracking
- Expansion joints: At structure interfaces and long intervals (30–60 m) with compressible filler
- Waterstops: PVC or rubber embedded across joints for seepage control
- Joint sealant: Polysulfide or polyurethane, tooled into saw-cut or formed groove

### Freeboard
Lined canal freeboard measured from design water surface to top of lining:
- USBR: 150 mm minimum for small canals, 300–600 mm for large
- Must account for wave action, operational surges, and measurement uncertainty

## Performance and Seepage Rates

**Typical seepage rates by lining type:**
- Unreinforced concrete (good condition): 0.5–2.0 L/m²/day
- Reinforced concrete: 0.3–1.0 L/m²/day  
- Geomembrane (HDPE): 0.01–0.1 L/m²/day
- Compacted clay (300 mm): 2–10 L/m²/day
- Unlined canal (sandy soil): 20–200 L/m²/day

## Lining Rehabilitation

Existing lining deterioration is common after 30–50 years. Rehabilitation options:
- Shotcrete overlay (30–50 mm) on existing concrete
- Geomembrane liner placed over existing concrete, covered with new concrete or soil
- Panel replacement for localized damage
- Crack repair with epoxy injection or routing and sealing
- Joint resealing
