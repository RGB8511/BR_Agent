# Standards and Regulatory References — Elk Basin Dam Safety Investigation

This document catalogs the regulatory requirements, testing standards, and engineering guidelines governing the Elk Basin Dam safety investigation, monitoring program, and remediation design. Standards are organized by category and include descriptions of how each standard applies to the Elk Basin Dam project.

---

## FERC Regulations

### 18 CFR Part 12 — Safety of Water Power Projects and Project Works

**Title**: Code of Federal Regulations, Title 18 (Energy), Part 12 — Safety of Water Power Projects and Project Works

**Issuing Authority**: Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC)

**Applicability to Elk Basin Dam**: Elk Basin Dam is regulated under FERC Project No. 4182. Part 12 establishes the mandatory framework for independent dam safety inspections, licensee engineering reports, and corrective actions for FERC-licensed hydroelectric projects. The following subparts are directly applicable:

- **18 CFR Part 12, Subpart A** — General provisions, definitions, and the requirement that licensees operate and maintain project works in a safe manner. Requires licensees to report any condition that may affect the safety of the project.
- **18 CFR Part 12D** — Independent Consultant (IC) inspection requirements. Requires FERC-licensed projects with dams above specified size thresholds to undergo comprehensive independent inspection at intervals not exceeding 5 years. For Elk Basin Dam, with a maximum height of 145 feet, the Part 12D inspection is mandatory. The October 2020 CDSC inspection was conducted under this requirement. The next Part 12D inspection is due by October 2025–2026.
- **18 CFR §12.10** — Requires licensees to file a formal engineer's report on safety of the dam within 6 months following a Part 12D inspection, responding to all items raised by the IC.
- **18 CFR §12.11** — Provisions for emergency action plans (EAPs): requires that an EAP be prepared, approved, and exercised for any FERC-licensed dam that poses a hazard to downstream life or property. Elk Basin Dam's EAP was last updated in 2019 and is due for revision following the 2025 investigation findings.

**Key requirement for Elk Basin Dam**: The 2020 Part 12D inspection classified Elk Basin Dam as Priority Action, requiring the licensee to implement a corrective action program within a specified schedule. The geotechnical investigation, enhanced monitoring, and remediation alternatives evaluation completed in 2025 constitute the licensee's response to this requirement.

### FERC Engineering Guidelines for the Evaluation of Hydropower Projects, Chapter 11 — Assessment of the Consequences of Dam Failure

**Title**: FERC Engineering Guidelines for the Evaluation of Hydropower Projects, Chapter 11: Assessment of the Consequences of Dam Failure

**Issuing Authority**: Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, Division of Dam Safety and Inspections

**Current Edition**: FERC Engineering Guidelines, April 2018 (with subsequent updates)

**Applicability to Elk Basin Dam**: Chapter 11 is the primary technical reference for dam safety evaluation of FERC-licensed projects. Key sections applicable to Elk Basin Dam:

- **Section 2 (Inspection Procedures)**: Visual inspection protocols for arch dams, embankment sections, and foundation drainage systems. Requires photographic documentation of all identified deficiencies. Applied during 2020 Part 12D inspection and 2025 investigation site visits.
- **Section 3 (Instrumentation and Monitoring)**: Guidelines for minimum instrumentation requirements, monitoring frequencies, and data evaluation. Specifies that seepage trends showing consistent increases require investigation and that turbid seepage is a potential internal erosion indicator requiring immediate evaluation.
- **Section 4 (Concrete Dam Evaluation)**: Structural evaluation procedures for arch and gravity dams, including FEM analysis requirements, allowable stress criteria, and evaluation of aging mechanisms including ASR. Requires that tensile demand not exceed tensile capacity (σ_t ≤ f't) for the governing load combination. Applied in the arch tensile screening (Equation 8 of this package).
- **Section 5 (Embankment Dam Evaluation)**: Slope stability analysis requirements for embankment sections including seismic evaluation. Specifies minimum factors of safety: FS ≥ 1.5 (static, normal pool), FS ≥ 1.0–1.1 (pseudo-static, 10%/50-yr seismic). Defines when Newmark displacement analysis is required (when pseudo-static FS < 1.2). Applied in right abutment slope stability analysis.
- **Appendix E (Slope Stability Analysis)**: Detailed guidance on analysis methods, material property selection, phreatic surface location, and acceptable FS thresholds. Recommends Spencer's method for composite failure surfaces.
- **Section 6 (Foundation Evaluation)**: Guidance on evaluating foundation drainage, grout curtain performance, and seepage control. Defines criteria for evaluating whether observed seepage rates constitute a safety concern.

## Concrete Testing Standards

### ASTM C42 — Standard Test Method for Obtaining and Testing Drilled Cores and Sawed Beams of Concrete

**Full Title**: ASTM C42/C42M — Standard Test Method for Obtaining and Testing Drilled Cores and Sawed Beams of Concrete

**Applicability**: Governs the core drilling, specimen preparation, and conditioning procedures for concrete core specimens recovered from the Elk Basin arch dam. Specifies minimum core diameter (not less than 3.7 times maximum aggregate size), length-to-diameter ratio requirements (L/D = 1.9–2.1 for compressive strength), end preparation (capping or grinding), and conditioning (moisture condition must be reported). All arch crown core specimens (Tables 10 and 11) were prepared and tested in accordance with ASTM C42 procedures.

**Key provisions applied at Elk Basin**: NX diameter (54 mm = 2.125 in) cores were used throughout; maximum aggregate size in the arch concrete is estimated at 1.5 inches, so the 2.125-inch core diameter is marginal relative to the 3.7× rule (would require 5.6-inch diameter). The laboratory report notes that the NX cores may underestimate strength by 5–10% due to the high aggregate size-to-core diameter ratio, and this uncertainty is incorporated in the structural evaluation.

### ASTM C150 — Standard Specification for Portland Cement

**Full Title**: ASTM C150/C150M — Standard Specification for Portland Cement

**Applicability**: The original construction cement specification for Elk Basin Dam referenced ASTM C150 Type II (moderate sulfate resistance, moderate heat of hydration), appropriate for mass concrete placement. Archived mill certificates from the 1966–1968 construction period were reviewed to obtain the total alkali content (Na₂O equivalent = 0.72%), which is critical to the ASR assessment. ASTM C150 requires reporting of total alkali content (Na₂O + 0.658 K₂O) and limits alkali content to 0.60% for "low-alkali" cement designation. The Elk Basin cement at 0.72% exceeds this threshold, confirming high-alkali cement as a contributing factor to ASR.

---

### ASTM C496 — Standard Test Method for Splitting Tensile Strength of Cylindrical Concrete Specimens

**Full Title**: ASTM C496/C496M — Standard Test Method for Splitting Tensile Strength of Cylindrical Concrete Specimens

**Applicability**: Governs the split tensile (Brazilian) test on cylindrical concrete core specimens. The test applies a line load along a diameter of the cylinder, inducing a relatively uniform tensile stress perpendicular to the loaded diameter. Split tensile strength f't is calculated as f't = 2P / (π × d × L). Applied to concrete core specimens from the arch crown to quantify tensile capacity of ASR-affected zones (Table 11). Results: f't = 295–345 psi (Zone 1, severe ASR); f't = 510 psi (Zone 4, sound interior). The split tensile results are used in the arch tensile screening criterion (Equation 8).

### ASTM C856 — Standard Practice for Petrographic Examination of Hardened Concrete

**Full Title**: ASTM C856 — Standard Practice for Petrographic Examination of Hardened Concrete

**Applicability**: The primary standard for petrographic examination of concrete, covering procedures for thin section preparation, polished section examination, and systematic identification of concrete constituents, paste microstructure, and deterioration features. ASTM C856 is the definitive standard for identifying and characterizing ASR in hardened concrete.

Applied to Elk Basin Dam arch crown core specimens. Petrographic examination confirmed: (1) rhyolitic volcanic glass and microcrystalline quartz as the reactive silica phases in the aggregate; (2) ASR gel in aggregate cracks, cement paste microcracks, and bleed channels in Zone 1 and Zone 2; (3) ettringite in air voids in all zones with severity decreasing from Zone 1 to Zone 4; (4) carbonation depth of 0.8 inches from downstream face; (5) crack width and frequency measurements consistent with the ASR severity zone classification. Petrographic report by a qualified concrete petrographer (licensed PE with ASTM C856 training) is included in the 2025 investigation report appendix.

## Rock Testing Standards

### ASTM D3967 — Standard Test Method for Splitting Tensile Strength of Intact Rock Core Specimens

**Full Title**: ASTM D3967 — Standard Test Method for Splitting Tensile Strength of Intact Rock Core Specimens

**Applicability**: Governs the Brazilian disk splitting tensile test on rock core specimens. Disk geometry (thickness approximately D/2) and loading rate (0.5–1.0 MPa/min) are specified. ITS is calculated as f't = 2P / (π × D × t). Applied to fresh granodiorite specimens to determine rock tensile strength for input to Hoek-Brown rock mass strength estimation and to evaluate foundation rock behavior under arch thrust loading. Results: ITS mean = 1,999 psi with range 1,806–2,207 psi across 10 specimens (Table 9). Only central splitting failures (Mode C) are accepted; irregular failures are rejected.

---

### ASTM D4767 — Standard Test Method for Consolidated Undrained Triaxial Compression Test for Cohesive Soils

**Full Title**: ASTM D4767 — Standard Test Method for Consolidated Undrained Triaxial Compression Test for Cohesive Soils

**Applicability**: Governs CU triaxial testing with pore pressure measurement for cohesive soil specimens. The method requires: specimen preparation from undisturbed samples (Shelby tubes), back-pressure saturation to B ≥ 0.95, consolidation at each confining pressure until primary consolidation is complete (t_50 method), and shearing at a rate slow enough to allow pore pressure equalization (rate based on t_50 from consolidation). Applied to embankment clay core (CL) and glacial till (GC) specimens to determine effective stress shear strength parameters (c', φ') for slope stability analysis. Results: clay core c' = 600 psf, φ' = 26°; glacial till c' ≈ 0, φ' = 34° (Table 6). Effective stress Mohr-Coulomb envelopes are used in Spencer's method slope stability analysis.

---

### ASTM D7012 — Standard Test Methods for Compressive Strength and Elastic Moduli of Intact Rock Core Specimens Under Varying States of Stress and Temperatures

**Full Title**: ASTM D7012 — Standard Test Methods for Compressive Strength and Elastic Moduli of Intact Rock Core Specimens Under Varying States of Stress and Temperatures

**Applicability**: Governs UCS testing (Method C, uniaxial compression) and triaxial compression testing of rock core specimens. Method C specifies specimen geometry (L/D = 2.0–2.5:1), loading rate (0.5–1.0 MPa/s), and measurement of axial and lateral strain (if elastic moduli are required). Applied to fresh granodiorite (9 specimens, Table 7) and weathered granodiorite (6 specimens, Table 8) specimens. Results: fresh granodiorite UCS mean = 22,422 psi, E50 mean = 8.46 × 10⁶ psi, ν = 0.24; weathered granodiorite UCS range 1,280–4,100 psi. These values are used for: (1) Hoek-Brown rock mass strength estimation, (2) FEM elastic modulus assignment to foundation elements, and (3) assessment of the bearing capacity of the arch foundation.

## Soil Testing Standards

### ASTM D4318 — Standard Test Methods for Liquid Limit, Plastic Limit, and Plasticity Index of Soils

**Full Title**: ASTM D4318 — Standard Test Methods for Liquid Limit, Plastic Limit, and Plasticity Index of Soils

**Applicability**: Governs Atterberg limits determination (LL, PL, PI) for fine-grained soils. Applied to embankment clay core specimens (B1-CC-1 through B1-CC-6, LL = 39–44, PI = 19–21, Table 4) and glacial till specimens (B4-GT-1 and B4-GT-2, LL = 26–28, PI = 11–12, Table 5). Atterberg limits are used for: (1) USCS soil classification, (2) compaction control reference (clay core compacted wet of optimum at 1–3% above OP), and (3) input to correlations for estimating undrained shear strength and coefficient of consolidation.

---

### ASTM D422 — Standard Test Method for Particle-Size Analysis of Soils

**Full Title**: ASTM D422 — Standard Test Method for Particle-Size Analysis of Soils (note: superseded by ASTM D7928 for hydrometer analysis, but D422 remains the reference for this project's pre-2025 testing; 2025 testing used both D422 and D7928)

**Applicability**: Governs gradation analysis (sieve analysis and hydrometer analysis) for soil specimens. Applied to glacial till specimens (Table 5) to characterize the gravel-sand-fines distribution (gravel 38–42%, sand 22–26%, fines 31–36%) and to filter/drain zone materials to confirm filter criteria compliance. Gradation data from borings B-1 and B-4 are used for USCS classification and for input to permeability correlations (Hazen's equation not applicable due to high fines content; falling-head permeability tests govern).

---

### ASTM D1557 — Standard Test Methods for Laboratory Compaction Characteristics of Soil Using Modified Effort

**Full Title**: ASTM D1557 — Standard Test Methods for Laboratory Compaction Characteristics of Soil Using Modified Effort (56,000 ft-lbf/ft³)

**Applicability**: Modified Proctor compaction test; governs determination of maximum dry density and optimum moisture content for compaction control. Applied to embankment clay core borrow material to establish the as-built compaction reference: maximum dry density = 106.9 pcf at optimum moisture content = 16.5%. Field dry density measurements (104.2–106.5 pcf) yield relative compaction values of 97.5–99.6%, confirming that the clay core was placed at or above 97% relative compaction as specified. High relative compaction confirms the low permeability measured in the laboratory (k = 2.8 × 10⁻⁷ cm/s) is representative of field conditions.

---

### ASTM D5084 — Standard Test Methods for Measurement of Hydraulic Conductivity of Saturated Porous Materials Using a Flexible Wall Permeameter

**Full Title**: ASTM D5084 — Standard Test Methods for Measurement of Hydraulic Conductivity of Saturated Porous Materials Using a Flexible Wall Permeameter

**Applicability**: Governs falling-head and constant-head permeability testing of soil specimens using a flexible-wall (triaxial-type) permeameter, which allows back-pressure saturation and measurement of small hydraulic conductivity values. Applied to trimmed clay core specimens from boring B-1 (specimen B1-CC-4) to measure hydraulic conductivity. Result: k = 2.8 × 10⁻⁷ cm/s, confirming effective seepage control. The flexible-wall method is preferred over rigid-wall for fine-grained soils because it prevents sidewall leakage that can artificially inflate measured permeability.

## Dam Safety Guidelines

### FERC Engineering Guidelines — Chapter 3: Inspection Procedures

**Applicability**: Provides detailed procedures for visual inspection of dam components including: inspection of concrete dam faces (cracking, efflorescence, staining, gel exudation for ASR), embankment inspection (seeps, sinkholes, slope distortion), foundation drainage inspection (seepage rates, turbidity, color), and instrumentation reading procedures. The systematic ASR inspection protocol in Chapter 3 was applied during both the 2020 Part 12D inspection and the 2025 investigation site visits to characterize map cracking extent (60% of downstream face) and gel exudation locations (25 sites).

---

### FERC Engineering Guidelines — Chapter 8: Seismic Ground Motion Analysis

**Applicability**: Provides guidance on probabilistic seismic hazard analysis (PSHA) and selection of design ground motion parameters for FERC-licensed projects. Requires use of the USGS National Seismic Hazard Model for site-specific PGA values. For Elk Basin Dam (Elmore County, Idaho, coordinates 43.52°N, 115.88°W), the 10%/50-year (475-year return period) PGA = 0.24g from the 2018 USGS NSHM. The pseudo-static seismic coefficient k_h = 0.12 (= 0.24g / 2.0) is derived per FERC Chapter 8 guidance. Chapter 8 also provides guidance on the Maximum Credible Earthquake (MCE) for use in dam breach consequence analysis.

---

### FERC Engineering Guidelines — Chapter 13: Geotechnical Investigations

**Applicability**: Provides guidance on planning and executing geotechnical investigations for dam safety evaluation, including: borehole spacing and depth requirements for detecting seepage paths, rock core logging procedures (RQD, ISRM weathering classification), packer permeability testing protocols, and Lugeon unit calculation. Applied in planning the 2025 boring program: boring spacing of 20–30 feet in the left abutment is consistent with Chapter 13 recommendations for characterizing a zone of concern (weathered rock seepage path). Post-grouting Lugeon testing target of ≤ 3 Lugeons is derived from Chapter 13 guidance for grouted foundation zones.

---

### USBR Design Standards No. 13 — Embankment Dams (Chapter 8: Seepage)

**Title**: USBR Design Standards No. 13 — Embankment Dams, Chapter 8: Seepage

**Issuing Authority**: U.S. Bureau of Reclamation

**Applicability**: USBR Design Standards No. 13 was the governing design standard for Elk Basin Dam construction (1966–1968), as USBR was the project designer. Chapter 8 provides the original design criteria for the embankment seepage control system: maximum allowable hydraulic gradient through the clay core, filter design criteria (filter rule D15/D85 and D15/D15), and drainage blanket capacity requirements. Current evaluation of the clay core's continuing adequacy (k = 2.8 × 10⁻⁷ cm/s vs. design specification) is made with reference to the original USBR standards to confirm that as-built conditions meet the original design intent.

### ICOLD Bulletin 107 — Dams and Related Structures in Cold Climate

**Title**: ICOLD Bulletin 107 — Dams and Related Structures in Cold Climate

**Issuing Authority**: International Commission on Large Dams (ICOLD)

**Applicability**: Provides guidance on the effects of cold climate on concrete dam durability, including the role of freeze-thaw cycling in accelerating ASR gel expansion and surface cracking. Elk Basin Dam experiences significant seasonal temperature variation (summer highs > 95°F, winter lows < 0°F) with frequent freeze-thaw cycles on the exposed downstream face. ICOLD Bulletin 107 informs the interpretation of ASR crack patterns and the design of the protective elastomeric coating recommended in the remediation plan.

### ACI 221R — Guide for Use of Normal Weight and Heavyweight Aggregates in Concrete

**Title**: ACI 221R — Guide for Use of Normal Weight and Heavyweight Aggregates in Concrete

**Issuing Authority**: American Concrete Institute (ACI)

**Applicability**: Provides guidance on identifying potentially reactive aggregates and controlling ASR in new concrete construction. ACI 221R Table 3.1 classifies aggregate types by reactivity; rhyolitic volcanic glass (present in Elk Basin aggregate) is listed as a highly reactive material. The standard recommends limiting cement alkali content to 0.40% Na₂O equivalent for highly reactive aggregates — well below the 0.72% Na₂O content of the Elk Basin construction cement. Applied retrospectively to characterize the severity of the ASR risk that was present in the original design and to contextualize the observed deterioration rate.

---

### ASTM C1260 — Standard Test Method for Potential Alkali Reactivity of Aggregates (Mortar-Bar Method)

**Full Title**: ASTM C1260 — Standard Test Method for Potential Alkali Reactivity of Aggregates (Mortar-Bar Method)

**Applicability**: Accelerated mortar bar test for screening aggregate reactivity. Expansion ≥ 0.20% at 14 days indicates potentially deleterious reactivity; ≥ 0.10% and < 0.20% is indeterminate. Applied retrospectively to quarry aggregate samples (not available from 1966 construction) during the 2025 investigation to confirm reactivity classification of the Elk Creek rhyolite aggregate. Results: 14-day expansion = 0.38%, confirming highly reactive classification. This result supports the conclusion that ASR was an inherent and foreseeable risk in the original design, given the combination of highly reactive aggregate and high-alkali cement.

---

### ACI 224R — Control of Cracking in Concrete Structures

**Title**: ACI 224R — Control of Cracking in Concrete Structures

**Issuing Authority**: American Concrete Institute (ACI)

**Applicability**: Provides guidance on types and causes of concrete cracking. ACI 224R Section 3.6 specifically addresses ASR-related cracking patterns and distinguishes map cracking due to ASR from cracking due to other mechanisms (alkali-carbonate reaction, freeze-thaw, plastic shrinkage, drying shrinkage, overload). The ACI 224R crack pattern description is consistent with the observed Elk Basin downstream face map cracking: random orientation, gel-filled crack surfaces, associated with aggregate particle reaction rims. This standard provides the technical basis for the ASR crack pattern interpretation in the 2025 investigation report.

---

### ICOLD Bulletin 165 — Alkali-Aggregate Reactions in Concrete Dams

**Title**: ICOLD Bulletin 165 — Alkali-Aggregate Reactions in Concrete Dams: Review and Recommendations

**Issuing Authority**: International Commission on Large Dams (ICOLD)

**Applicability**: The primary international reference for ASR in dam structures. Covers: (1) mechanisms and kinetics of ASR in large concrete structures; (2) diagnostic methods including petrographic examination (ASTM C856), expansion monitoring, and core testing; (3) assessment of structural consequences for gravity, buttress, and arch dams; (4) remediation options including slot cutting, lithium treatment, surface treatment, and monitoring; (5) case histories from international dams. The ASR assessment methodology, strength reduction characterization, and lithium treatment recommendation for Elk Basin Dam are all consistent with the ICOLD Bulletin 165 framework.
