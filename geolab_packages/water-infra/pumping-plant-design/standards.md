# Standards Reference — Pumping Plant Design

## 10 States Standards — Recommended Standards for Water Works (GLUMRB)
**Scope:** Great Lakes–Upper Mississippi River Board standards adopted by many states. Chapter on pumping covers station layout, redundancy requirements, suction and discharge piping, standby power, and controls. Sets minimum requirements for public water system pump stations.

## Hydraulic Institute HI 9.8 — Rotodynamic Pumps for Pump Intake Design
**Scope:** Definitive standard for pump intake (wet well) design. Approach velocity limits, minimum submergence, bay dimensions, floor and wall clearances, anti-vortex devices, and physical model testing criteria. Essential for avoiding vortex and air entrainment problems.

## NFPA 820 — Standard for Fire Protection in Wastewater Treatment and Collection Facilities
**Scope:** Fire and explosion protection for facilities handling water/wastewater. Ventilation requirements, electrical classification of hazardous areas, fire detection and suppression. Applicable to enclosed pump stations, especially those handling raw water or wastewater.

## NFPA 110 — Standard for Emergency and Standby Power Systems
**Scope:** Installation, testing, and maintenance of emergency and standby power systems. Generator sizing, fuel storage, automatic transfer switches, and testing requirements. Applicable to all critical pump stations requiring standby power.

## OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146 — Permit-Required Confined Spaces
**Scope:** Occupational safety for confined space entry. Below-grade pump stations, wet wells, and valve vaults are confined spaces requiring entry permits, atmospheric monitoring, rescue provisions, and training. Critical for station design — provide means to avoid confined space entry where possible.

## ASHRAE Handbook — HVAC Applications (Chapter on Enclosed Vehicular Facilities / Industrial)
**Scope:** Heating, ventilation, and cooling design for industrial spaces including pump rooms. Heat dissipation calculations, air change rates, temperature control, and humidity management for motor-driven equipment.

## Sanks et al. (1998) — Pumping Station Design (3rd ed.)
**Scope:** Comprehensive design reference covering all aspects of pumping station engineering: pump selection, piping design, structural, electrical, controls, surge analysis, noise/vibration, and life-cycle cost analysis. The industry bible for pump station design.
