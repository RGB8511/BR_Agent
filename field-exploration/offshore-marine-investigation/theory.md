# Offshore & Marine Geotechnical Investigation

## Overview

Marine geotechnical investigation serves ports, harbors, bridges, pipelines, cables, offshore platforms, wind farms, and coastal structures. The marine environment imposes unique constraints: vessel motion, water depth, current/wave forces, limited visibility, remote operations, and high daily costs ($50K–$500K+/day depending on vessel class).

## Investigation Platforms

### Jack-Up Barge
Self-elevating platform with legs (spud cans) that penetrate the seabed. Provides stable platform for drilling in shallow water (< 70–100 m). Standard approach for nearshore/harbor investigations, bridge foundations, and shallow-water wind farms.

**Advantages:** Stable platform (no vessel motion), can support conventional drill rigs, accessible to crews, equipment laydown.

**Limitations:** Water depth limited, requires suitable seabed for spud can foundation (no boulders, not too soft), transit speed very slow, mobilization expensive.

### Geotechnical Drill Ship / Vessel
Dynamically-positioned (DP) or anchored vessel with moon pool and heave-compensated drill string. Required for deep water (> 100 m). Vessels range from small multipurpose to dedicated geotechnical vessels (Fugro, GeoSea, Gardline).

**Heave compensation:** Active or passive systems that maintain drill string position despite vessel heave (wave-induced vertical motion). Critical for sample quality and CPT data quality. Modern systems achieve ±50 mm compensation.

### Barge / Pontoon
Flat-deck work barge with anchors. Low cost for sheltered water (harbors, rivers, lakes). Limited by wave conditions — typically < 1 m significant wave height. Jack-up pontoons available for small projects.

### Diver-Operated / ROV
For seabed sampling and in-situ testing without a drill string. Diver-operated in shallow water (< 40 m); ROV-operated for deeper. Used for seabed CPT systems, grab samples, and visual inspection.

## Marine Geophysical Methods

### Bathymetry (Multibeam Echo Sounder — MBES)
High-resolution seabed topography. Swath width = 2–5 × water depth. Resolution to 0.1 m in shallow water. Detects: seabed features, slopes, obstructions, scour, bedforms, debris. Foundation of all marine survey work.

### Side-Scan Sonar (SSS)
Acoustic imaging of seabed surface. Provides plan-view mosaic showing texture, objects, rock outcrops, sand waves, debris, and cables/pipelines. Complementary to MBES — shows surficial character rather than topography.

### Sub-Bottom Profiler (SBP)
Acoustic penetration below seabed. Multiple frequency ranges:
- **Pinger (2–12 kHz):** Moderate resolution, 10–50 m penetration in soft sediments
- **Chirp (1–10 kHz swept):** Good resolution (0.1–0.3 m) + moderate penetration (20–50 m)
- **Boomer (0.5–5 kHz):** Higher energy, coarser resolution, penetrates stiffer soils/thin rock
- **Sparker (0.1–1 kHz):** Highest energy, deepest penetration (50–200+ m), lower resolution
- **Parametric SBP (e.g., Innomar):** Narrow beam, high resolution (0.05–0.1 m), good in shallow water

Reveals: stratigraphy, buried channels, rock head, gas, faults, mass transport deposits, paleosurfaces.

### Marine Seismic Reflection
Multi-channel reflection for deeper structure (> 50 m sub-bottom). Used for regional geology, fault mapping, and reservoir-scale investigations. Air gun or sleeve gun sources; towed hydrophone streamer. Processing: CMP stacking, migration, velocity analysis.

### Marine Seismic Refraction
Ocean-bottom seismometers (OBS) or towed systems. Determines seismic velocity structure and depth to rock. Used for dredging assessments (rippability), pipeline route surveys, and regional geology.

## Marine Sampling Methods

### Gravity Corer
Free-fall weighted barrel that penetrates seabed under gravity. Typical: 3–6 m penetration in soft sediments, 50–100 mm diameter liner. Simple, reliable, inexpensive. Limited to soft clay and silt — poor penetration in sand.

### Vibrocorer
Vibrating head on a core barrel (up to 6–12 m penetration). Electric or hydraulic vibrator clamps to the core barrel head. Penetrates sand, silt, and stiff clay better than gravity corer.

**Sample quality:** Vibration disturbs granular structure — not suitable for undisturbed samples in sand. Acceptable for classification and index testing.

### Piston Corer (Jumbo/Giant)
Long-barrel gravity corer (10–30+ m) with internal piston that reduces friction and maintains suction. Kullenberg-type. Recovers long continuous cores in soft to firm marine clays.

**Applications:** Deep-water site investigations, academic/research, pipeline route characterization.

### Box Corer
Large box (typically 500 × 500 × 500 mm) dropped to seabed, closes, and recovers intact seabed surface including the water-sediment interface. Preserves stratification, bioturbation, and surficial sediment for geochemistry, biology, and engineering index testing.

### Grab Sampler
Clamshell or van Veen grab — scoops surface sediment. Disturbed sample. Used for environmental assessment, grain size, and contamination sampling. Not for geotechnical engineering purposes.

### Downhole Sampling (from Drill String)
Standard geotechnical sampling tools (thin-wall push, piston, Shelby tube) deployed through a drill string from vessel or jack-up. Same quality standards as onshore, with complications from heave and water depth. Heave-compensated samplers critical for quality.

## Marine In-Situ Testing

### Seabed CPT (Cone Penetration Test)
Self-contained CPT system deployed on seabed frame (e.g., Fugro Seascout, Neptune, A.P. van den Berg ROSON). Frame sits on seabed, pushes cone into soil hydraulically. Typical: 3–30+ m penetration depending on system and soil.

**Advantages:** No borehole needed, continuous profile, pore pressure dissipation possible, high quality data.

### Downhole CPT
CPT cone deployed through a drill string. Can test to any depth by alternating drilling and testing. Standard for deep offshore investigations. Heave-compensated push systems required for data quality.

### T-bar and Ball Penetrometer
Full-flow penetrometers (cylindrical bar or sphere) that provide continuous strength profiles in very soft deepwater clays. Advantage over CPT: exact theoretical solutions exist (no empirical Nkt factor needed). Cyclic T-bar testing provides remolded strength and sensitivity.

### In-Situ Vane (Offshore)
Deployed through drill string. Same principle as onshore but with remote operation. Measures peak and remolded undrained shear strength. Being largely replaced by full-flow penetrometers in deepwater practice.

## Project Types and Investigation Scope

### Offshore Wind Farms
Extensive investigation: geophysical survey of entire site + export cable route, geotechnical boreholes/CPT at each turbine location (or representative subset), cable route CPT/vibrocores. Standards: BSH, DNV-ST-0126, SUT guidelines.

### Marine Pipelines and Cables
Route survey: bathymetry, SSS, SBP along corridor. Geotechnical: CPT/vibrocores at intervals along route (typically 1–5 km for pipelines, 5–20 km for cables). Pipeline burial assessment: soil strength, trenching assessment, on-bottom stability.

### Port and Harbor
Jack-up or barge-based investigation. Borings with SPT/sampling/CPT at berth and breakwater locations. Bathymetric survey, side-scan for obstructions. Dredging assessment: UCS, point load for rock, grab/vibrocore for sediment.

### Offshore Platforms (Oil & Gas)
Most intensive investigation: detailed geophysical survey, multiple deep boreholes (100+ m sub-seabed), continuous CPT, advanced sampling (STACOR piston corer), in-situ testing (pressuremeter, T-bar). ISO 19901-8 governs requirements.
