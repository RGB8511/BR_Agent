# Soil Sampling Methods & Sample Quality

## Sample Categories

### Disturbed Samples
Soil structure destroyed; moisture content may or may not be preserved. Suitable for: grain-size analysis, Atterberg limits, classification, moisture content, specific gravity, organic content, chemical testing. Not suitable for strength, compressibility, or permeability testing.

**Sources:** Split-spoon (SPT), auger cuttings, bulk samples from test pits, bag samples, wash samples.

### Undisturbed Samples
Soil structure, moisture content, density, and stress history preserved as closely as possible. Required for: consolidated undrained triaxial, consolidation (oedometer), permeability, CU/UU strength, advanced laboratory testing.

**Truth:** No sample is truly undisturbed. The process of drilling, inserting a sampler, extracting the sample, transporting, and extruding inevitably causes some disturbance. The goal is to minimize disturbance to acceptable levels for the intended testing.

## Disturbed Sampling Methods

### Split-Spoon Sampler (SPT Sampler)
Standard 51 mm (2 in) OD, 35 mm (1.375 in) ID split-barrel sampler driven with the SPT hammer. Recovers a disturbed but representative sample.

**Sample quality:** Heavily disturbed — high area ratio (~112%), dynamic driving destroys structure. Useful for: classification, moisture content, visual identification.

### Bulk / Bag Samples
Large quantities from auger cuttings, test pits, or excavations. Used for compaction testing (Proctor), CBR, grain size of coarse materials. Bag in sealed containers with moisture preserved if needed.

## Undisturbed Sampling Methods

### Thin-Wall (Shelby) Tube Sampler (ASTM D1587)
Standard undisturbed sampler for cohesive soils. Thin-wall seamless steel or brass tube pushed (not driven) into soil at the bottom of the borehole.

**Standard dimensions:** 76 mm (3 in) OD × 73 mm (2.875 in) ID × 760 mm (30 in) long. Area ratio ~10–13%. Cutting edge sharpened to reduce disturbance.

**Procedure:** Lower tube to cleaned bottom of borehole. Push smoothly (no hammering) using hydraulic feed at steady rate. Push 610 mm, leave 150 mm for waste soil at top. Wait 5–10 minutes for sample to grip tube (adhesion). Rotate to shear bottom. Extract carefully.

**Limitations:** Only effective in soft to stiff cohesive soils. Not suitable for sand (sample falls out), gravel, very stiff/hard clay (can't push tube), or sensitive soils (pushing still causes significant disturbance).

### Fixed-Piston Sampler (Osterberg Type)
Piston locked at bottom of tube during lowering, preventing soil from entering during placement. When tube is pushed past the piston (piston remains stationary), it creates suction that helps retain the sample and prevents swelling. Top of the line for soft clays.

**Advantages:** Prevents borehole debris from entering tube before sampling. Suction from piston retains soft samples. Prevents sample expansion during extraction. Best quality undisturbed samples in soft to medium clays.

**Disadvantages:** More complex and expensive. Requires skilled operator. Limited push force available.

### Denison / Pitcher Barrel Sampler
Double-tube core barrel with a spring-loaded inner tube that extends ahead of the rotating outer barrel. The inner tube pushes into the soil (like a Shelby tube) while the outer barrel cuts around it. Designed for stiff to hard clay, silt, and soft rock where Shelby tube push is insufficient but rotary coring would disturb the sample.

### Block Sampling
Hand-cut cubical block from test pit wall or excavation face. The highest quality sample possible — minimal disturbance, known orientation. Typically 200–300 mm cube, wrapped in wax and cheesecloth.

**Limitations:** Only practical in cohesive soils above the water table, where test pits are feasible. Labor-intensive. Limited to shallow depths.

### Gel-Push Sampling (GP-S)
Japanese-developed technique using polymer gel lubricant between the sample and tube wall, dramatically reducing wall friction and sample disturbance. Produces exceptional quality samples in soft to firm clays and silts. Gaining international acceptance.

### Sherbrooke Block Sampler
Large-diameter (250 mm) block sampler lowered into a borehole. Cutting shoe carves cylindrical block in situ. Recovers high-quality undisturbed samples from below the water table where hand-cut blocks are impossible.

## Sample Disturbance Assessment

### Lunne et al. (1997) Criteria
Disturbance assessed by comparing the void ratio change during reconsolidation in the oedometer:

Δe/e₀ = (e₀ - e_after_reconsolidation) / e₀

where e₀ = initial void ratio, e_after_reconsolidation = void ratio after reconsolidating to estimated in-situ stress.

Lower Δe/e₀ = less disturbance.

### Volumetric Strain Criterion
εvol = Δe/(1+e₀) during reconsolidation. Disturbed samples show higher volumetric strain because the sampling process destructured the soil, which then recompresses under the in-situ stress.

## Sample Handling and Preservation

### Immediately After Recovery
- Seal tube ends with expandable packers and wax/tape, or with O-ring caps
- Label: project, boring number, sample number, depth, date, sampler type, top/bottom orientation
- Store upright (top up) in padded container
- Protect from vibration, shock, freezing, and excessive heat
- Transport to laboratory as soon as practical

### In Laboratory
- Store in temperature-controlled room (±4°C of field temperature; never freeze)
- Extrude carefully in direction of sampling (push from bottom)
- Test within 2 weeks for sensitive clays; within 1–3 months for general soils
- Never store indefinitely without testing — sample quality degrades with time
