# Clearwater Valley Pumping Plant PP-2 — Geotechnical Investigation & Performance

**Package ID:** projects.clearwater-valley.pumping-plant-pp2
**Report Reference:** WGC Report 2018-GEO-0093, October 2018; WGC Field Inspection Memo March 2019; CVID 2024 Annual Facility Assessment; CVID Preliminary Design Memo TK-2A July 2025
**Location:** Cottonwood Creek, Elmore County, Idaho
**Owner:** Clearwater Valley Irrigation District (CVID)

---

## Project Description

Pumping Plant PP-2 is a high-capacity irrigation pump station constructed for the Clearwater Valley Irrigation District (CVID) along the east bank of Cottonwood Creek in Elmore County, Idaho. The facility delivers supplemental irrigation supply to approximately 4,200 acres of agricultural land in the Clearwater Valley service area.

The facility consists of three (3) vertical-turbine pump units, each rated at 500 horsepower (HP), with a combined design capacity of 15,000 gallons per minute (GPM) at a total dynamic head of 185 feet. Water is drawn from Cottonwood Creek via a submerged intake structure and discharged into the CVID main conveyance canal approximately 0.4 miles to the east.

The primary structures included in the geotechnical investigation scope are:

- **Pump Bay / Wet Well Structure:** Reinforced concrete below-grade structure, approximately 28 ft × 42 ft in plan, with a maximum excavation depth of 18 ft below existing grade. Supports three pump column pedestals and associated mechanical equipment. Founded on drilled shafts socketed into Challis Volcanic bedrock.
- **Motor Control Center (MCC) Building:** Single-story masonry-clad steel frame building, approximately 22 ft × 36 ft in plan, supporting electrical switchgear, motor starters, and SCADA equipment. Founded on conventional spread footings within the upper alluvial sand stratum.
- **Discharge Header Vault:** Below-grade concrete vault housing 24-inch diameter discharge headers, check valves, and pressure gauges. Depth approximately 8 ft. Founded on compacted structural fill over alluvial sand.
- **Access Road and Site Improvements:** Gravel-surfaced access road, approximately 800 linear feet, with culvert crossings at two drainage swales.

The geotechnical investigation was performed by Western Geotechnical Consultants (WGC) under Contract No. CVID-2018-004 between June and August 2018. The investigation report (WGC Report 2018-GEO-0093, dated October 2018) formed the basis for foundation design by the structural engineer of record.

Construction was completed in late 2019. A construction verification memo was issued by WGC in March 2019 following field inspection of foundation excavations and drilled shaft installation. A five-year performance assessment was conducted by CVID in 2024.

## Regional Geology

The PP-2 site lies within the Western Snake River Plain physiographic province, a northwest-trending structural graben that has been progressively filled with Neogene and Quaternary sediments and volcanic deposits since the late Miocene.

### Challis Volcanic Group

The dominant bedrock formation beneath the site is the **Challis Volcanic Group** (Eocene to Oligocene age, approximately 45–37 Ma). The Challis Volcanics in this region consist of a complex sequence of andesitic to rhyolitic lava flows, tuffs, and volcaniclastic breccias erupted from a series of calderas and stratovolcanoes in central Idaho. At the PP-2 site, the exposed and subsurface rock is primarily a **welded rhyolitic ash-flow tuff** (ignimbrite) with sparse phenocrysts of quartz and sanidine in a devitrified glassy to cryptocrystalline groundmass.

Challis Volcanic rocks in the project area are variably fractured and weathered. Near the surface the rock is highly weathered (Grade IV–V) with oxidized joint surfaces and clay-filled discontinuities. At depths below 4–5 ft into fresh rock, the material is moderately to slightly weathered (Grade II–III), exhibiting moderate to high unconfined compressive strengths.

Structural fabric within the Challis Volcanics at this site is dominated by two subvertical joint sets oriented approximately N20°E/80°NW and N75°W/72°NE, intersecting at approximately 55°. A third, gently dipping (15°–25°) foliation surface approximately parallel to the original flow banding is present sporadically and does not represent a dominant sliding surface for the foundation geometry. RQD values in fresh rock range from 45% to 78%, indicating fair to good quality rock mass.

### Quaternary Alluvium

Overlying the Challis Volcanics is a sequence of **Quaternary alluvial and fluvial deposits** associated with Cottonwood Creek and its predecessors. These deposits range in age from late Pleistocene to Holocene and record multiple cycles of cut-and-fill related to climatic fluctuations and base-level changes in the Snake River. The alluvial sequence at this site consists of:

1. **Upper alluvial clay / overbank deposit** (late Holocene): Soft to medium stiff lean clay with silt (CL-ML) deposited during flood events. Contains trace fine sand laminae and occasional organic matter. Thickness 4–12 ft.
2. **Lower alluvial sand and gravel** (Pleistocene–Holocene transition): Medium dense silty fine to medium sand with gravel (SM) representing channel or bar deposits. Thickness 8–12 ft.

The alluvial sequence is relatively thin compared to other Snake River Plain sites due to the proximity of the Challis Volcanic basement highs in this portion of Elmore County. Lateral continuity is good across the site, though the clay layer thins from approximately 12 ft in the southwestern portion of the site to approximately 4 ft in the northeastern corner.

### Fill and Surficial Materials

The upper 2–4 ft of the soil profile consists of existing fill and topsoil disturbed by prior agricultural activities. The fill is variable in composition (SM/GM) and contains occasional gravel, cobbles, and construction debris from a historical irrigation structure removed in approximately 1995.

## Subsurface Profile

The subsurface profile at PP-2 was defined based on four (4) geotechnical borings (BH-PP2-01 through BH-PP2-04) advanced during the 2018 investigation. A four-layer stratigraphic model was established:

### Layer 1 — Fill and Topsoil (0 to 4 ft bgs)

- **USCS Classification:** SM (Silty Sand) to GM (Silty Gravel with Sand)
- **Description:** Variable fill and topsoil; dark brown to grayish brown; loose to medium dense; contains gravel, cobbles, and trace debris from prior grading activities.
- **Thickness:** 2–4 ft (averages approximately 3.2 ft across site)
- **SPT N-values:** 4–8 blows/ft (loose, disturbed state; not suitable for design)
- **Engineering Use:** Stripped and replaced with structural fill prior to foundation construction. Not considered a bearing stratum.

### Layer 2 — Alluvial Clay (4 to 12 ft bgs)

- **USCS Classification:** CL (Lean Clay with Sand) — consistently classified across all borings
- **Description:** Stiff to very stiff reddish-brown to tan lean clay; moist to wet near groundwater table; contains trace fine sand laminae; exhibits moderate plasticity.
- **Thickness:** 6–10 ft (average 7.8 ft)
- **Index Properties:**
  - Natural moisture content: 18–24% (average 20.4%)
  - Liquid limit (LL): 35–41 (average 38.2)
  - Plasticity index (PI): 17–20 (average 18.6)
  - Activity (A = PI / % clay): 0.82–0.95 (inactive to normal)
  - Linear shrinkage: 8–11%
- **SPT N-values:** 9–16 blows/ft (stiff to very stiff; increases with depth)
- **Swell Characteristics:** Free swell 2.8–4.1% at initial moisture content; swell pressure 1,050–1,620 psf. Classified as moderately expansive per WRI swell potential chart. Moisture barrier and over-excavation mitigation specified.
- **Consolidation:** Preconsolidation pressure (σ'p) = 4,000–5,800 psf. OCR = 4.0–5.2 (overconsolidated, consistent with desiccation). Compression index Cc = 0.11–0.15; recompression index Cr = 0.012–0.018.
- **Corrosion:** Moderate corrosion potential (resistivity 3,800–5,100 Ω·cm; pH 7.2–8.1; sulfate 280–420 ppm; chloride 180–310 ppm).

### Layer 3 — Alluvial Sand (12 to 23 ft bgs)

- **USCS Classification:** SM (Silty Sand) to ML (Sandy Silt) — transition zone at base
- **Description:** Medium dense to dense brown to gray silty fine to medium sand with trace gravel; moist to saturated below water table; generally uniform in gradation.
- **Thickness:** 8–13 ft (average 10.5 ft); pinches out in northeast corner of site where bedrock is shallowest
- **Index Properties:**
  - Natural moisture content: 14–19% (above water table); 22–27% (below water table)
  - Fines content: 18–32% (SM classification range)
  - D50: 0.18–0.28 mm
- **SPT N-values:** 17–29 blows/ft (medium dense to dense)
- **Engineering Use:** Provides adequate bearing for spread footings (MCC building). Liquefaction analysis indicates FS > 1.5 at all depths for the design seismic event.

### Layer 4 — Bedrock: Challis Volcanics (20.5 to 23 ft bgs to total boring depth)

- **Rock Type:** Welded rhyolitic ash-flow tuff (ignimbrite), Challis Volcanic Group
- **Depth to Top of Rock:** 20.5 ft (BH-PP2-04, northeast) to 23.0 ft (BH-PP2-01, southwest)
- **Weathering:** Grade IV–V (highly weathered) at top-of-rock transition, transitioning to Grade II–III within 2–4 ft below top of rock.
- **RQD:** 45–78% (fair to good rock mass quality) in fresh rock below weathered zone
- **Core Recovery:** 62–91% (average 78%)
- **Unconfined Compressive Strength (UCS):** 5,180–8,680 psi (mean 6,640 psi) on NX core specimens
- **Indirect Tensile Strength (ITS):** 540–890 psi (mean 685 psi) via Brazilian tensile test
- **Elastic Modulus (E50):** 1.2–2.8 × 10⁶ psi (average 1.9 × 10⁶ psi)
- **Poisson's Ratio (ν):** 0.22–0.28
- **Engineering Use:** Primary bearing stratum for drilled shaft end-bearing and socket friction. Allowable end-bearing capacity 20,000 psf; allowable side friction 215 psi.

## Groundwater Conditions

### 2018 Investigation Measurements

Groundwater was encountered in all four borings during drilling. Open standpipe piezometers were installed in BH-PP2-02 and BH-PP2-03 and monitored at 24-hour and 72-hour intervals following boring completion. Stabilized groundwater depths at the time of investigation (July–August 2018, active irrigation season) were:

- **BH-PP2-01:** 13.8 ft bgs (instrument not installed; observed during drilling)
- **BH-PP2-02:** 13.5 ft bgs (72-hr stabilized piezometer reading)
- **BH-PP2-03:** 14.0 ft bgs (72-hr stabilized piezometer reading)
- **BH-PP2-04:** 14.2 ft bgs (72-hr stabilized piezometer reading)

Average groundwater depth during 2018 investigation: **13.9 ft bgs** (elevation approximately 2,847.1 ft NAVD88).

### Seasonal Variation and Aquifer Characteristics

The shallow unconfined aquifer at the site is hosted within the Alluvial Sand layer (Layer 3) and is hydraulically connected to Cottonwood Creek. Groundwater elevations are strongly influenced by:

1. **Seasonal stream stage fluctuations:** Cottonwood Creek stage rises approximately 2.5–3.5 ft during spring snowmelt runoff (April–June), transmitting a groundwater mounding signal that reaches the site with an estimated 10–14 day lag.
2. **Irrigation canal operations:** CVID operates the main canal from approximately April 1 through October 15. Seepage from unlined canal reaches and check structures contributes recharge, elevating the local water table by an estimated 1.5–2.5 ft above non-irrigation season levels.
3. **Precipitation:** Annual precipitation at the site averages approximately 12 inches; direct recharge contribution is minor relative to creek and canal sources.

Seasonal variation is estimated at ±2 ft around the annual mean. The design high groundwater elevation used for dewatering and structural design was established at **12 ft bgs** (elevation 2,849 ft NAVD88) to represent an approximate 2-ft rise above the 2018 summer measurements.

### 2024 Performance Monitoring

CVID personnel conducted two manual piezometer readings in fall 2024 as part of the annual facility assessment:

- **October 2024 (post-irrigation season):** 15.8 ft bgs (BH-PP2-02 piezometer)
- **November 2024 (pre-dormant season):** 16.1 ft bgs (BH-PP2-02 piezometer)

These readings, approximately 1.6–1.9 ft below the 2018 summer measurements, are consistent with the expected seasonal pattern (low water table in fall following canal shutdown). No anomalous piezometric responses or artesian conditions have been observed. The piezometer in BH-PP2-03 was found damaged and not readable in 2024; replacement is recommended.

### Dewatering Design Basis

Foundation excavation for the pump bay wet well required dewatering to a minimum depth of 18 ft bgs. Design high groundwater at 12 ft bgs required approximately 6 ft of drawdown over an excavation footprint of approximately 28 × 42 ft. A single-stage wellpoint dewatering system with four wellpoints per side (16 total) was specified at 5 ft spacing around the perimeter, with a header manifold and vacuum-assisted discharge pump. Estimated dewatering flow rate: 35–65 GPM based on estimated hydraulic conductivity of the alluvial sand (k ≈ 4 × 10⁻³ cm/s from SPT-based correlation). No significant settlement of adjacent structures was anticipated due to dewatering; the nearest existing structure (CVID headworks building) is approximately 180 ft from the excavation edge.

## Laboratory Testing Program

The laboratory testing program was designed to characterize each stratigraphic unit for foundation design, construction specification, and long-term performance evaluation. Testing was performed at WGC's Boise laboratory, which maintains AASHTO accreditation for all test methods listed.

### Moisture Content (ASTM D2216)

Thirteen (13) moisture content determinations were performed on representative split-spoon and Shelby tube samples. Results span all four stratigraphic layers. Moisture contents in Layer 2 (Alluvial Clay) averaged 20.4%, within the expected range for CL soils near the plastic limit. Layer 3 (Alluvial Sand) showed expected increase in moisture content below the groundwater table.

### Particle Size Distribution (ASTM D6913 / D7928)

Six (6) combined sieve and hydrometer analyses were performed on samples from Layers 1 through 3. Results confirmed USCS classifications assigned in the field and provided grain size data for liquefaction susceptibility assessment and filter design. Fines content (passing No. 200 sieve) ranged from 18–32% for SM materials in Layer 3, confirming moderate fines content potentially susceptible to liquefaction without density correction.

### Atterberg Limits (ASTM D4318)

Seven (7) Atterberg limit determinations were performed on fine-grained samples from Layer 2. Results consistently classified the material as CL per USCS (A-line on Casagrande chart, LL 35–41, PI 17–20). Activity values of 0.82–0.95 indicate the clay mineral is likely a mixture of illite and montmorillonite, consistent with the volcanic source rock weathering products common to the Snake River Plain region.

### Compaction (ASTM D698)

One (1) standard Proctor compaction test was performed on representative Layer 2 material blended with clean sand to simulate anticipated structural fill borrow. Results: maximum dry density 115.2 pcf at optimum moisture content 13.8%. This result informed the structural fill specification (95% of standard Proctor MDD at ±2% of optimum) used for backfill around the pump bay walls and beneath the MCC spread footings.

### One-Dimensional Swell and Consolidation (ASTM D4546 / D2435)

Two (2) one-dimensional swell/consolidation tests were performed on undisturbed Shelby tube samples from the alluvial clay. Initial specimens were loaded to in-situ vertical stress before inundation to measure free swell under simulated wetting. Swell test procedures followed ASTM D4546 Method B (constant volume measurement of swell pressure, followed by axial loading to determine swell/consolidation curve). Results identified the clay as moderately expansive and defined preconsolidation pressures consistent with desiccation overconsolidation.

### Corrosion Testing (ASTM G57 / D4972)

Three (3) soil samples from the alluvial clay were tested for corrosion characteristics relevant to buried concrete and metallic structures:
- Soil electrical resistivity (ASTM G57, Wenner 4-electrode method)
- Soil pH (ASTM D4972, pH meter method)
- Water-soluble sulfate content (ASTM C1580)
- Water-soluble chloride content (EPA Method 300.0)

Results indicated moderate corrosion potential per NACE RP-0169 criteria, driving the structural specification for Type II (moderate sulfate-resistant) Portland cement with w/c ≤ 0.45 and minimum 3-inch concrete cover for all buried concrete elements.

### Unconfined Compressive Strength (UCS) — ASTM D7012 Method C

Twelve (12) UCS tests were performed on NX-size (54.7 mm diameter) rock core specimens recovered from the two cored borings (BH-PP2-03 and BH-PP2-04). Core specimens were trimmed to nominal L/D ratios of 2.0–2.5 per ASTM D7012 requirements. Axial strain was measured with LVDTs to allow determination of E50 and Poisson's ratio. Failure modes were recorded as axial splitting (brittle failure at high UCS) or shear fracture (lower UCS specimens with pre-existing discontinuities). UCS results ranged from 5,180–8,680 psi (mean 6,640 psi), consistent with moderately strong to strong rock per ISRM classification.

### Indirect Tensile Strength (ITS) — ASTM D3967

Fourteen (14) Brazilian disc tensile tests were performed on NX core discs trimmed to L/D ≈ 0.5 per ASTM D3967. ITS results ranged from 540–890 psi (mean 685 psi), giving a mean UCS/ITS ratio of approximately 9.7, consistent with brittle igneous and volcanic rock types.

### Rock Quality Designation (RQD) — ASTM D6032

RQD was measured on all recovered core from seven (7) core runs in BH-PP2-03 and BH-PP2-04. RQD was computed as the cumulative length of pieces ≥ 4 inches (100 mm) divided by the total core run length, expressed as a percentage. RQD ranged from 45–78% in fresh rock (fair to good quality), with the lower values associated with the upper weathered transition zone and with zones of sub-horizontal flow contacts within the tuff.

## Foundation Design

### Design Philosophy and Approach

Foundation design for PP-2 was governed by two primary challenges: (1) the moderately expansive alluvial clay in Layer 2, which poses uplift and differential movement risk to lightly loaded or slab-on-grade elements, and (2) the need for deep, reliable support for the heavily loaded pump bay wet well that must accommodate large cyclic hydraulic loads and equipment vibration. The design solution employed two distinct foundation types:

- **Drilled shafts** for the pump bay/wet well structure, socketed into Challis Volcanic bedrock to mobilize high end-bearing and socket friction capacity
- **Spread footings** for the MCC building, bearing on the medium-dense alluvial sand below the expansive clay

### Drilled Shaft Design — Pump Bay

Six (6) drilled shafts were designed to support the pump bay wet well structure. Design parameters:

- **Shaft diameter:** 24 inches (0.61 m)
- **Top of rock (design):** 22 ft bgs (average across site, conservative)
- **Socket depth into fresh rock:** 5 ft minimum (L/D = 2.5)
- **Total shaft length:** approximately 27 ft
- **Allowable end-bearing capacity (q_b):** 20,000 psf, derived from mean UCS of 6,640 psi with a factor of safety FS = 3.0 applied against the bearing mode failure (q_b = UCS × 144 / FS × correction factor)
- **Allowable side friction (f_s):** 215 psi (1.49 tsf), derived as f_s = 0.15 × UCS_mean / FS with FS = 2.0 and an empirical reduction factor of 0.15 for rough socket in welded tuff
- **Casing requirement:** Temporary casing through full depth of alluvial soils and weathered rock; casing to be pulled during concrete placement (trémie or pump)
- **Minimum concrete strength:** f'c = 4,000 psi at 28 days; Type II cement; w/c ≤ 0.45
- **Concrete cover (clear):** 3 inches minimum to rebar cage

Individual shaft capacity verification was performed by WGC during construction using rock surface inspection and hand penetrometer readings on soil immediately above rock.

### Spread Footing Design — MCC Building

Spread footings for the MCC building bearing in the Alluvial Sand (Layer 3) were designed for a net allowable bearing pressure of **3,000 psf**. Design parameters:

- **Bearing stratum:** Alluvial Sand (SM), Layer 3, at approximately 12–14 ft bgs
- **Net allowable bearing pressure (q_a):** 3,000 psf (controlled by settlement, not bearing failure; FS against shear failure ≈ 5)
- **Terzaghi bearing capacity parameters:** N_c = 25.8, N_q = 14.7, N_γ = 11.2 for φ = 28°
- **Ultimate bearing capacity (q_ult):** approximately 15,700 psf for a 5 ft × 5 ft footing at 14 ft depth
- **Factor of safety against bearing failure:** q_ult / q_a = 5.2 (adequate)
- **Minimum footing embedment:** 5 ft below base of alluvial clay (approximately 17 ft bgs) to eliminate influence of expansive clay on footing performance
- **Footing dimensions:** Column footings 4 ft × 4 ft to 5 ft × 5 ft; continuous wall footings 2.5 ft wide

### Settlement Analysis

**Pump Bay (Drilled Shafts):** Settlement is expected to be negligible, as load transfer is primarily to end-bearing on rock. Elastic compression of the shaft and bedrock socket was estimated at less than 0.1 inch under full design load. No measurable consolidation settlement of underlying rock is anticipated.

**MCC Building (Spread Footings):** Settlement analysis used the elastic formula S = qB(1-ν²)/E × I_f with the following parameters:
- Applied stress q = 3,000 psf
- Footing width B = 4.5 ft (average)
- Young's modulus of SM sand E = 700,000–1,100,000 psf (estimated from SPT N-values via Bowles correlation)
- Poisson's ratio ν = 0.30
- Influence factor I_f = 0.85 (flexible footing, L/B = 1)

**Predicted total settlement:** 0.75–1.25 inches (mean 0.95 in)
**Predicted differential settlement:** 0.3–0.5 inches across MCC footprint (angular distortion approximately 1/500, within ACI 318 limits)

**Measured Performance (2024):** Maximum measured settlement 0.22 inches at the southwest corner of the MCC building (most heavily loaded column); average settlement 0.16 inches. Differential settlement 0.07 inches (angular distortion approximately 1/5,100). Settlement stabilized after approximately 18 months of operation, consistent with immediate elastic response of the dense alluvial sand.

## Lateral Earth Pressures

Lateral earth pressure design parameters were established for the pump bay below-grade walls and the discharge header vault. The following design values apply to the alluvial soil profile (combined Layers 1–3) for design of below-grade concrete walls:

### Design Parameters

| Parameter | Symbol | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Effective friction angle | φ' | 28° | Direct shear on reconstituted CL/SM samples |
| Effective cohesion | c' | 200 psf | Residual cohesion in alluvial clay; conservative for long-term |
| Moist unit weight | γ | 120 pcf | Average of Layers 1–3 |
| Saturated unit weight | γ_sat | 126 pcf | Based on Gs = 2.68, average void ratio |
| Active earth pressure coefficient | Ka | 0.35 | Rankine: Ka = tan²(45 – φ/2) |
| At-rest earth pressure coefficient | K0 | 0.50 | Jaky: K0 = 1 – sin(φ) |
| Passive earth pressure coefficient | Kp | 2.90 | Rankine: Kp = tan²(45 + φ/2) = 1/Ka |

### Lateral Pressure Distribution

For permanent below-grade walls (pump bay and vault):

- **At-rest condition (no wall movement, rigid construction):** σ_h = K0 × σ'_v + u, where u = hydrostatic pressure below GWT
- **Active condition (flexible walls or assumed for conservatism):** σ_h = Ka × σ'_v – 2c'√Ka + u
- **Surcharge loading:** Uniform surcharge of 300 psf applied at grade for equipment and vehicles during operation

The design lateral earth pressure diagram for the pump bay walls used at-rest pressure (K0 = 0.50) in recognition of the relatively rigid cast-in-place concrete construction that limits wall deflection below the threshold required to mobilize active conditions.

Passive resistance behind the vault walls was mobilized for overturning stability checks; a reduction factor of 0.6 was applied to the full Kp value to limit passive mobilization to a serviceability displacement.

### Liquefaction Assessment

Simplified liquefaction analysis (Youd et al. 2001 method, per ASCE 7-16 requirements for Site Class D) was performed for the alluvial sand (Layer 3). Key inputs and results:

- **Design earthquake:** Mw = 6.5, PGA = 0.15g
- **SPT N-values used:** Clean sand corrected N₁(60)cs = 22–34 blows/ft
- **Cyclic Resistance Ratio (CRR):** 0.22–0.41 (increases with depth due to higher N-values and increasing overburden)
- **Cyclic Stress Ratio (CSR):** 0.11–0.14 (adjusted for Mw = 6.5)
- **Factor of Safety against liquefaction (FS_liq = CRR/CSR):** 1.57–2.94 (all layers > 1.5)

**Conclusion:** The alluvial sand at PP-2 is not considered liquefiable under the design seismic event. The relatively dense state (N > 17 throughout), moderate PGA (0.15g), and thin liquefiable layer thickness contribute to the favorable result.

### Seismic Foundation Design

No seismic uplift or sliding instability was identified for the drilled shaft foundations. The at-rest lateral earth pressure envelope was increased by 25% for out-of-plane seismic loading on the pump bay walls per ASCE 7-16 Section 11.8.

## Swell Mitigation

### Swell Potential Classification

The alluvial clay (Layer 2, CL, LL = 35–41, PI = 17–20) was classified as **moderately expansive** per the WRI (1965) swell potential classification chart based on PI and clay fraction (38% < 2 micron). Free swell under simulated field wetting ranged from 2.8–4.1% at natural moisture content. Swell pressure measured at constant volume was 1,050–1,620 psf (average 1,340 psf).

The expansive clay poses the greatest risk to lightly loaded or slab-on-grade elements where structural loads do not exceed the swell pressure. For the heavily loaded pump bay (drilled shaft loads >> swell pressure) the risk is negligible. For the MCC building spread footings bearing at 17 ft bgs (below the alluvial clay), swell forces could be transmitted upward through backfill if non-expansive fill is not used.

### Mitigation Measures

The following swell mitigation measures were specified and implemented:

1. **Over-excavation:** All footing subgrades within the footprint of structures underlain by expansive clay were over-excavated a minimum of 2 ft below design bearing elevation and backfilled with non-expansive compacted granular fill (structural fill meeting specifications below).

2. **Non-expansive structural fill specification:**
   - Material: Pit-run sand and gravel, USCS GW, GP, SW, or SP
   - Fines content (passing No. 200): ≤ 5%
   - Liquid limit (if tested): ≤ 25
   - Plasticity index: non-plastic (NP)
   - Maximum particle size: 4 inches
   - Compaction: ≥ 95% Standard Proctor MDD at OMC ± 2%

3. **Moisture barriers:** 10-mil polyethylene vapor barriers installed beneath all slabs-on-grade (MCC floor slab, equipment pad) with 12-inch lap splices and continuous perimeter seal to prevent post-construction wetting of expansive soils beneath floor slabs.

4. **Grade beam void forms:** 4-inch deep cardboard void forms (Cartonfoam or equal) installed beneath all grade beams bearing within the zone of potential swell. Void forms provide clearance for clay swell without transmitting uplift forces to the structural frame.

5. **Drainage:** Perimeter drainage aggregate (12-inch wide × 18-inch deep clean gravel with perforated HDPE pipe) installed at the base of all exterior walls to divert surface infiltration away from building subgrade and minimize post-construction moisture changes.

### Performance Observations (2024)

CVID personnel and structural inspector observed the following swell-related items during the 2024 assessment:
- Void form clearance (grade beams): 3–4 inches of remaining void space visible through an access port at the MCC northwest corner, confirming that swell has not yet consumed the design void allowance.
- No measurable heave of floor slabs or grade beams.
- No cracking of concrete slabs attributable to expansive soil movement.
- Perimeter drainage functioning as designed (clear effluent observed from outlet).

## Corrosion Protection

### Site Corrosion Assessment

Corrosion testing of three soil samples from the alluvial clay stratum yielded the following results (full data in tables.json):

| Test | Result Range | NACE Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Soil resistivity (Ω·cm) | 3,800–5,100 | Moderately corrosive |
| Soil pH | 7.2–8.1 | Slightly alkaline, low corrosion risk |
| Water-soluble sulfate (ppm) | 280–420 | Exposure Class S1 (ACI 318-19 Table 19.3.3.1) |
| Water-soluble chloride (ppm) | 180–310 | Moderate; supplemental protection warranted |

Sulfate concentrations in the range 200–1,500 ppm correspond to ACI 318 Class S1 (moderate sulfate exposure), requiring Type II (MS) or Type V cement or SCM blend with equivalent sulfate resistance.

Chloride concentrations at 180–310 ppm are below the threshold for severe attack on embedded steel (2,000 ppm water-soluble or 0.10% by weight of cement per ACI 318 Section 26.4.2.1), but supplement precautions are appropriate given the below-grade and partially saturated exposure conditions.

The overall corrosion designation was classified as **moderately corrosive** per NACE RP-0169.

### Protection Specifications

The following corrosion protection specifications were incorporated into the structural design:

**Concrete:**
- **Cement type:** ASTM C150 Type II (moderate sulfate resistance) for all buried or below-grade concrete
- **Maximum water-to-cement ratio:** w/c ≤ 0.45 for all below-grade concrete
- **Minimum concrete cover (clear):** 3 inches for all buried elements; 2 inches for interior (above-grade) elements
- **Supplementary cementitious materials:** Fly ash (Class F) at 20% replacement by mass of cementitious content, providing additional sulfate resistance and reducing heat of hydration for mass drilled shaft pours

**Steel and Metal:**
- **Reinforcing steel:** Standard ASTM A615 Grade 60 for interior elements; epoxy-coated rebar (ASTM A775) for all reinforcing steel in elements with direct soil contact or in the tidal/splash zone of the wet well
- **Mechanical equipment (pumps, impellers, column pipe):** Stainless steel (316L) or engineered coatings per equipment manufacturer specification
- **Structural steel (anchor bolts, embedded plates):** Hot-dip galvanized (ASTM A153) for buried elements; zinc-rich primer + epoxy topcoat for above-grade elements in wet well environment
- **Check valves and gate valves:** Bronze trim (AWWA C515 or C516); epoxy-lined ductile iron body
- **Discharge piping:** Cement-mortar lined ductile iron (AWWA C104) for buried segments; 304 stainless steel for wet well connections

## Construction Verification

### WGC Field Inspection — March 2019

WGC geotechnical staff conducted a two-day construction observation and field inspection of the PP-2 foundation excavations and drilled shaft installation on March 14–15, 2019 (WGC Field Inspection Memo, dated March 22, 2019). Key observations and verifications are summarized below.

### Excavation Observations

Foundation excavation for the pump bay wet well was completed to a final bottom elevation of approximately 18 ft bgs using a tracked excavator. The following observations were made:

- **Groundwater:** Active dewatering was underway at the time of inspection using the specified 16-wellpoint system. Groundwater was successfully depressed to approximately 20–21 ft bgs (approximately 2 ft below excavation bottom), allowing dry working conditions. Dewatering flow rate estimated at 42 GPM, within the predicted range.
- **Soil conditions at excavation bottom:** The excavation bottom exposed the upper Alluvial Sand layer (Layer 3) at an elevation consistent with boring predictions. Soils appeared medium dense and dry to moist. No soft or unstable zones were observed.
- **Alluvial clay condition:** The alluvial clay (Layer 2) was exposed in the excavation walls at 4–12 ft depth. The clay was firm to stiff with no signs of disturbance or sloughing. Temporary shoring (soldier pile and lagging) was in place and functioning.

### Rock Surface Inspection

After advancing to rock in each shaft hole, WGC geologist inspected the recovered cuttings and hand-augered/probed the shaft bottom to verify rock quality. Observations:

- **Top-of-rock depths:** 20.5–22.8 ft bgs, consistent with boring predictions (22 ft average used in design).
- **Rock quality at shaft bases:** Sound, fresh to slightly weathered rhyolitic tuff in all 6 locations. No soft seams or adversely oriented discontinuities observed at shaft bases.
- **Hand penetrometer readings on clay above rock:** 2.5–3.5 tsf at depth 18–20 ft bgs in shaft cuttings, confirming very stiff alluvial clay (consistent with design assumption of no skin friction contribution from clay).

### Drilled Shaft Installation

Six (6) drilled shafts (S-1 through S-6) were installed on March 14–15, 2019 using a rotary drilling rig (Bauer BG 18H). Key data:

- **Shaft diameter:** 24 inches (confirmed with caliper)
- **Socket depths into fresh rock:** 5.0–6.2 ft (all exceeded 5.0 ft minimum)
- **Total shaft depths:** 26.5–29.0 ft bgs
- **Concrete placement:** Pump-placed (tremie not required due to successful dewatering) with slump 6–8 inches confirmed by inspector
- **Concrete batch tickets:** Type II cement, Class F fly ash at 20%, w/c = 0.42 (verified on tickets)
- **Rebar cage inspection:** Cage dimensions and epoxy coating confirmed prior to placement
- **Groundwater in shafts at time of concrete:** None in 5 of 6 shafts; minor seepage (< 1 GPM) in S-4 at socket bottom; judged acceptable

### Structural Fill Compaction

Structural fill placement for over-excavation areas and backfill was monitored by WGC field technician. Nuclear density gauge testing (ASTM D6938) was performed at the following compaction levels:

- **MCC footing bearing pad (over-excavation backfill):** 98.8% of Standard Proctor MDD (maximum dry density 115.2 pcf)
- **Pump bay wall backfill (exterior):** 96.2–97.4% (average 96.8%) of Standard Proctor MDD
- **Discharge vault backfill:** 97.1% of Standard Proctor MDD

All compaction results met or exceeded the specified minimum of 95% Standard Proctor MDD. No failing tests were recorded.

## Proposed TK-2A Surge Tank

### Background

CVID's 2025 capital planning process identified a hydraulic surge (water hammer) risk associated with the sudden shutdown of two or more PP-2 pump units during power outages. A preliminary hydraulic transient analysis (CVID internal, May 2025) estimated maximum transient pressure surges of 85–120 psi at the pump discharge manifold under worst-case two-pump trip conditions. The existing air release / vacuum break valves provide insufficient attenuation. A hydropneumatic surge tank is proposed to limit transient pressures to ≤ 60 psi and prevent column separation in the 24-inch discharge main.

### TK-2A Proposed Configuration

- **Tank designation:** TK-2A (Surge Tank, Pumping Plant PP-2)
- **Type:** Welded steel tank, vertical cylindrical, open-top with sealed overflow connection
- **Capacity:** 250,000 gallons (effective surge volume 120,000 gallons minimum per hydraulic analysis)
- **Diameter:** 40 feet
- **Height (shell):** 28 feet
- **Design standard:** AWWA D100 (Welded Carbon Steel Tanks for Water Storage)
- **Material:** ASTM A36 carbon steel shell and bottom plate; internal epoxy lining (AWWA C210)
- **Location:** Approximately 85 feet northeast of the PP-2 pump bay, on previously undisturbed alluvial soils

### Foundation Type — Ringwall

A concrete ringwall foundation is proposed for TK-2A, consistent with AWWA D100 Section 14 (Foundation Design) for large welded steel tanks. The ringwall bears on the Alluvial Sand (Layer 3) at the TK-2A location, where the clay layer is anticipated to be approximately 5–7 ft thick based on extrapolation of boring data.

**Preliminary design parameters (subject to refinement after supplemental investigation):**

| Parameter | Preliminary Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Shell bearing pressure (ringwall) | 4,500 psf | Tank shell dead load + water load distributed to ringwall |
| Allowable bearing pressure (net) | 3,000 psf | Same as PP-2 MCC design; Layer 3 sand |
| Ringwall width | 3.0 ft | Required to reduce bearing to ≤ 3,000 psf |
| Ringwall depth to bearing | 14 ft bgs | Below base of expansive clay (anticipated) |
| Seismic design basis | ASCE 7-16, Risk Category II | Storage tank, non-critical service |
| Anchor bolt design | AWWA D100 Section 4.4, overturning check |  |
| Tank empty overturning check | FS = 1.5 minimum | Wind + seismic per AWWA D100 |
| Total tank weight (full) | 10.5 MN (2,360 kips) | Water weight 8.8 MN + steel 1.7 MN |

### Settlement Estimate (Preliminary)

Preliminary settlement estimate for TK-2A ringwall using elastic settlement formula with same soil parameters as PP-2 MCC:

- Applied net stress under ringwall: 4,500 psf (shell) – 120 pcf × 14 ft = 4,500 – 1,680 = 2,820 psf net
- Estimated total settlement: 0.8–1.5 inches (preliminary; sensitive to clay thickness at TK-2A location)
- Differential settlement (ring): 0.3–0.6 inches (tilt ≤ 1/500 tank diameter, within AWWA D100 limits)

### Supplemental Investigation Program

Prior to final design, a supplemental geotechnical investigation is recommended:

1. Two (2) additional borings (BH-TK2A-01 and -02) at diametrically opposite points on the proposed tank footprint, advanced to minimum 40 ft bgs or 15 ft into fresh bedrock
2. Undisturbed Shelby tube sampling of clay at TK-2A location for swell testing
3. One (1) additional corrosion sampling profile at TK-2A location
4. Groundwater monitoring: Install 2-inch standpipe piezometer in each boring; read at 48 hours and 7 days

**Anticipated geotechnical findings:** Clay layer expected to be thinner at TK-2A location (northeast corner of site, consistent with boring thinning trend). Bedrock at approximately 18–20 ft bgs. Groundwater similar to 2018 measurements.
