# Canal Structures — Checks, Drops, Turnouts & Crossings

## Overview

Canal systems require numerous structures to control water levels, dissipate energy from elevation changes, divert water to users, cross natural and man-made obstacles, and measure flow. The USBR "Design of Small Canal Structures" is the foundational reference for irrigation canal structures in the western United States.

## Check Structures

Check structures maintain upstream water levels for turnout delivery. They create a controlled backwater by partially obstructing the canal.

### Types
- **Fixed weir checks:** Simple concrete weirs with flashboards or stoplogs for level adjustment. Low cost, limited flexibility.
- **Radial gate checks:** Tainter gates allowing continuous level adjustment. Common on larger canals.
- **Overshot gates:** Hinged leaf that automatically maintains upstream level. Self-regulating — popular for modernized canals.
- **Long-crested weirs:** Labyrinth or duckbill weirs that provide stable upstream levels with minimal head variation over a range of flows.

### Design Criteria
- Must pass the full canal capacity with acceptable headloss
- Maintain upstream level within ±0.15 m (±0.5 ft) of target for turnout delivery
- Spacing: every 1.5–5 km depending on canal slope and delivery requirements
- Stilling downstream if check creates supercritical flow at partial openings

## Drop Structures

When canal alignment requires a steeper descent than the canal design slope, drop structures dissipate excess energy.

### Vertical (Rectangular) Drops
- Free overfall into a stilling pool
- USBR Type VI impact basin for small drops (< 1.8 m head, < 2.8 m³/s)
- Pool depth sized by momentum balance (sequent depth for hydraulic jump)
- Drop height limited to approximately 3–4.5 m per structure

### Baffled Drops (USBR)
- Baffled apron with rows of staggered concrete blocks
- Energy dissipated by flow impacting baffles progressively
- No tailwater depth required — self-adjusting energy dissipation
- Slope typically 2H:1V. Suitable for drops up to 6 m.

### Chute Drops
- Concrete-lined chute on natural slope, with stilling basin at toe
- Used for larger drops where vertical drops would be impractical
- Design follows spillway chute principles (critical depth at crest, supercritical flow in chute, hydraulic jump in basin)

### Pipe Drops
- Closed conduit carrying water from upper to lower canal level
- Pipe drop combines drop function with road crossing
- Inlet transitions from canal to pipe, pipe descends, energy dissipation at outlet

## Turnouts and Division Structures

### Turnouts (Delivery Gates)
Divert water from the canal to a lateral or farm delivery:
- **Pipe turnout:** Gated pipe through the canal bank. Simplest form.
- **Constant-head orifice (CHO):** Maintains constant head across the orifice for accurate flow measurement. USBR standard design.
- **Open-channel turnout:** Side weir or gated opening into a lateral canal.

### Division Structures
Split flow between continuing canal and major lateral:
- Typically gated on both legs for flow control
- May incorporate measurement structures
- Radial gates or slide gates depending on size

### Flow Measurement at Turnouts
- **Constant-head orifice:** Q = Cd × A × √(2g × Δh). Most accurate when properly maintained.
- **Parshall flume:** Standard measurement device in canals. Free-flow and submerged-flow equations.
- **Broad-crested weir:** Simple, robust, lower accuracy.
- **Propeller meters:** For pipe deliveries.

## Canal Crossings

### Inverted Siphons
Carry canal water under obstacles (roads, rivers, depressions):
- Pipe(s) dip below the canal invert, pressurized by the head difference
- Inlet and outlet transitions from open channel to pipe
- Minimum velocity ≥ 1.0 m/s (3.3 ft/s) to prevent sediment deposition
- Air vent at high point of each barrel
- Trashrack at inlet
- Head loss = friction + entrance + exit + bend losses

### Elevated Flumes / Aqueducts
Carry canal water over obstacles (valleys, streams, roads):
- Concrete or steel trough on piers
- Open-channel flow maintained — no pressurization
- Used when the canal is higher than the obstacle

### Overchutes / Drain Crossings
Carry drainage or storm water over (overchute) or under (drain inlet) the canal:
- **Overchute:** Storm drainage crosses over the canal in an elevated conduit/flume
- **Culvert under canal:** Storm drainage passes through a culvert beneath the canal prism. Must be structurally adequate for canal embankment load.

### Road Crossings
- **Bridge:** Road passes over the canal
- **Culvert/siphon:** Canal passes through a culvert under the road. Pipe sized for full canal capacity with acceptable head loss.

## Wasteways and Safety Structures

### Wasteways
Discharge excess water from the canal:
- **Side-channel wasteway:** Overflow weir on the canal bank discharging to a natural drain
- **Siphon wasteway:** Automatic siphon that activates when canal level rises above a set elevation
- **Gated wasteway:** Manual or automatic gates for controlled spill

### Safety Considerations
- Wasteways spaced to prevent canal overtopping from storm inflow, operational errors, or check gate failure
- Combined capacity of all wasteways downstream of each check should equal or exceed canal capacity
- Public safety: fencing, warning signs, escape ladders in concrete-lined canals, animal escape ramps
