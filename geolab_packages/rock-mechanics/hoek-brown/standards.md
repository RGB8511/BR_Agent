# Standards Reference — Hoek-Brown Criterion

## ASTM D7012 — Standard Test Methods for Compressive Strength and Elastic Moduli of Intact Rock Core Specimens under Varying States of Stress and Temperatures

**Scope:** Determines σ_ci (UCS), E_i, and ν for intact rock. Also covers triaxial compression for m_i determination.

**Methods:**
- Method C: Uniaxial compressive strength with deformation measurement (σ_ci, E_i, ν)
- Method D: Triaxial compressive strength at specified confining pressures

**Key Requirements for σ_ci:**
- L/D ratio: 2.0–2.5 (standard 2.0; correct if different)
- End flatness: ≤ 0.025 mm
- End perpendicularity: ≤ 0.25° from specimen axis
- Loading rate: 0.5–1.0 MPa/s (stress controlled) — must achieve failure in 2–15 minutes
- Minimum 5 specimens for reliable average; 10+ preferred for statistical analysis
- Report individual values, mean, standard deviation

**For m_i Determination (Triaxial):**
- Minimum 5 confining pressures spanning 0 to 0.5σ_ci
- Suggested: σ₃ = 0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.35, 0.50 × σ_ci
- Fit Hoek-Brown curve to σ₁ vs. σ₃ data using least-squares regression
- Solve for m_i and σ_ci simultaneously (the fitted σ_ci may differ from UCS test value)

**Sample Quality:**
- Fresh, unweathered, representative specimens
- No visible fractures through specimen (testing intact rock, not rock mass)
- Core stored to prevent moisture loss and temperature changes
- NX (54.7 mm) or larger diameter preferred

## ISRM Suggested Methods

The International Society for Rock Mechanics provides suggested methods for:
- Uniaxial compressive strength (1979, updated)
- Triaxial compression (1983)
- Deformability (1979)
- Tensile strength — Brazilian test
- Point load strength index

These are harmonized with but not identical to ASTM methods. ISRM suggested methods are widely used internationally, particularly in mining and tunneling practice.

## Note on GSI

GSI is NOT a measured parameter and has no associated standard test method. It is estimated from geological observation using the GSI chart. The quality of GSI estimation depends heavily on the experience of the engineering geologist and the quality/extent of rock exposure available for observation.

For core-only sites (no exposures), GSI can be estimated from RQD and joint condition, but this is less reliable than direct observation of rock face structure.
