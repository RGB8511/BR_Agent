# Standards Reference — Discontinuity Shear Strength

## ASTM D5607 — Standard Test Method for Performing Laboratory Direct Shear Strength Tests of Rock Specimens Under Constant Normal Force

**Scope:** Laboratory direct shear test on rock discontinuities (natural joints, bedding planes) or intact rock.

**Procedure:**
1. Prepare matched pair of rock specimens containing the discontinuity
2. Encase in plaster/grout/epoxy in shear box halves — discontinuity aligned with shear plane
3. Apply constant normal force
4. Shear at constant displacement rate (typically 0.1–0.5 mm/min)
5. Record shear force and displacement (horizontal and vertical/dilation)
6. Repeat at 3+ normal stress levels for strength envelope

**Key Requirements:**
- Specimen size: ≥ 50 mm × 50 mm (larger preferred for rough joints — 100 × 100 or 150 × 150 mm)
- Normal stress range: should bracket expected field stress conditions
- Shear rate: slow enough for drained conditions in rock
- Record both peak and residual strength
- Measure dilation (vertical displacement) during shearing — critical for understanding joint behavior
- Photograph and describe joint surface before and after testing (roughness, infilling, matching)

**Data Reporting:**
- Shear stress vs. shear displacement plots for each normal stress
- Peak and residual shear stress vs. normal stress (strength envelopes)
- Normal displacement (dilation) vs. shear displacement
- JRC back-calculated from peak envelope if using Barton-Bandis interpretation
- Description of failure surface: through asperities, along surface, or through infilling

**Notes:**
- Sample preparation critical — avoid damaging the joint surface
- Specimen orientation must be documented (shear direction relative to roughness features)
- For infilled joints, preserve the infilling during preparation
- Test under saturated conditions if in-situ joint will be saturated
- Constant normal stiffness (CNS) version available for confined conditions (underground) but not covered by D5607

## ISRM Suggested Method for Determining Shear Strength (1974, 2014 Update)

Similar to D5607 but includes:
- Recommendations for multistage testing (one specimen, multiple normal stresses — less preferred but useful when specimens are scarce)
- Guidance on constant normal stiffness (CNS) testing for underground applications
- Tilt test procedure for JRC estimation
- Schmidt hammer procedure for JCS estimation
