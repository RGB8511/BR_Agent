# Test Pits, Trenches & Shallow Exploration Methods

## Overview

Test pits and trenches are the most direct method of subsurface investigation — they expose soil and rock in situ for visual examination, logging, sampling, and testing. They are faster and cheaper than drilling for shallow investigations and provide three-dimensional exposure impossible to obtain from boreholes.

## Excavation Methods

### Backhoe Pits
The workhorse of shallow exploration. Standard backhoe (tracked or rubber-tired) excavates to 3–5 m (10–15 ft) depth in most soils. Large excavators can reach 6–8 m (20–25 ft).

**Advantages:** Fast (15–30 min per pit), inexpensive, continuous exposure of soil profile, allows bulk sampling, visual assessment of stratigraphy and structure, direct measurement of layer thicknesses. Can detect features invisible in boreholes — lenses, pinch-outs, lateral variation, fill/native contact, buried structures, voids.

**Limitations:** Depth limited by equipment reach and groundwater. Cannot excavate below water table without dewatering. Requires access for heavy equipment. Disturbance zone around excavation. Safety considerations (trench collapse).

### Test Trenches
Long linear excavations to expose continuous cross-sections. Used for fault investigations (paleoseismic trenches), dam foundation mapping, borrow source evaluation, and landfill investigations.

**Typical dimensions:** 1–3 m wide, 2–5 m deep, 10–100+ m long. Shored or sloped per OSHA requirements.

**Paleoseismic trenches:** Specifically designed to cross suspected fault traces. Logged in detail with grid system, photographed, radiocarbon-dated. May be 3–5 m deep and 50+ m long. USGS/CGS/USBR protocols.

### Large-Diameter Borings (Bucket Auger)
Drilled shafts 600–1500 mm (24–60 in) diameter, allowing personnel entry for direct inspection and sampling at depth. Can reach 30+ m.

**Applications:** Deep inspection of bearing surfaces for drilled shaft foundations, direct observation of rock quality, hand sampling of undisturbed materials at depth, in-situ testing on exposed surfaces.

**Requirements:** Casing through unstable soils, forced ventilation, gas monitoring (confined space entry), minimum 750 mm diameter for personnel entry. OSHA permit-required confined space protocols.

### Hand Excavation / Auger
Manual auger (Iwan, Dutch) for shallow (< 3 m) reconnaissance. Used in areas inaccessible to equipment — steep slopes, dense vegetation, wetlands, archaeological sites. Also hand-dug test pits in developing countries.

## Logging Exposed Faces

### Grid System
Establish horizontal and vertical grid (typically 0.5 m or 1 ft spacing) on the exposed face using string lines or paint marks. Reference all features to grid coordinates. Photograph with scale bar and grid visible.

### Documentation Requirements
- **Soil/rock description:** USCS classification, color (Munsell), moisture, consistency/density, structure, inclusions, odor
- **Stratigraphy:** Layer contacts (sharp, gradational, irregular, erosional), dip/strike of contacts, lateral continuity
- **Discontinuities:** Fractures, joints, shear zones, slickensides — orientation, spacing, persistence, infilling, roughness
- **Groundwater:** Seepage locations, flow rates, water table position (if encountered)
- **Special features:** Roots, organics, fill materials, debris, voids, cementation, mottling, oxidation/reduction zones
- **Photographs:** Overall face, detail shots of key features, all with scale references

### Photogrammetry and LIDAR
Modern practice increasingly uses photogrammetric mapping of trench faces — create 3D models from overlapping photographs. Structure-from-Motion (SfM) with consumer cameras or drones. LIDAR scanning for precise geometry. Produces permanent digital record superior to hand logs for complex exposures.

## Sampling from Test Pits

### Bulk (Disturbed) Samples
Large-volume samples (20–100+ kg) for laboratory testing — grain size, Atterberg limits, compaction (Proctor), moisture content. Collected from specific horizons with shovel or backhoe bucket.

### Block Samples
Hand-carved undisturbed blocks for high-quality strength and consolidation testing. Carved from the pit face or floor, sealed with wax, and carefully transported. Block size typically 200–300 mm cube. The highest quality undisturbed sampling method available.

**Procedure:** Excavate pedestal of soil on pit floor or shelf on pit wall. Trim to block shape with knife/wire saw. Seal immediately with multiple layers of wax and cheesecloth. Label orientation. Pack in rigid container with padding.

### In-Situ Testing in Pits
- **Hand vane shear** on exposed faces or pit floor
- **Sand cone / nuclear gauge** density on pit floor or bench
- **Plate load test** on pit floor at proposed bearing elevation
- **Field permeability** — falling head in pit (crude) or constant head with standpipe
- **DCP (Dynamic Cone Penetrometer)** through pit floor

## Safety — OSHA Excavation Standard (29 CFR 1926 Subpart P)

### Classification of Soil
- **Type A:** Cohesive with qu ≥ 144 kPa (1.5 tsf). Clay, silty clay, hardpan.
- **Type B:** Cohesive with 48 < qu < 144 kPa. Silt, sandy loam, medium clay. Also previously disturbed Type A, fissured material, dry unstable rock.
- **Type C:** Granular soils (gravel, sand, loamy sand), submerged soil, soil from which water is freely seeping. qu < 48 kPa.

### Maximum Allowable Slopes (unshored)
- Type A: 0.75H:1V (53°) — max 6 m (20 ft) depth
- Type B: 1H:1V (45°) — max 6 m depth
- Type C: 1.5H:1V (34°) — max 6 m depth

### Shoring Requirements
Trenches > 1.5 m (5 ft) deep require protective system: sloping/benching, shoring (timber, aluminum hydraulic), shielding (trench box). Competent person must inspect daily and after rain events.

### Additional Requirements
- Spoil pile ≥ 0.6 m (2 ft) from edge
- Means of egress (ladder/ramp) within 7.6 m (25 ft) of all workers
- No heavy equipment operating near unshored trench edge
- Barricades around open excavations
- Utility locates (call before you dig — 811 in US)
- Atmospheric testing if hazardous atmosphere suspected
