# Groundwater Monitoring — Well Installation, Development & Measurement

## Monitoring Well Purpose

Monitoring wells provide access to the subsurface for measuring groundwater levels (piezometric head), collecting water quality samples, performing hydraulic tests (slug tests, pump tests), and long-term observation of groundwater conditions. Proper installation is critical — a poorly constructed well provides misleading data.

## Well Construction Components

### Borehole Drilling
Borehole advanced by hollow-stem auger (most common for shallow monitoring wells in soil), mud rotary (deeper wells, rock), air rotary, or sonic drilling. Borehole diameter typically 150–250 mm (6–10 in) for 50 mm (2 in) monitoring wells; larger for pump test wells.

### Well Screen
Slotted or wire-wound section that allows groundwater to enter the well while retaining aquifer material (with filter pack).

**Screen selection:**
- Slot size: selected to retain 85–100% of filter pack material (for artificial filter pack) or 40–60% of formation material (for naturally developed wells)
- Screen length: spans the target monitoring zone. Short screens (1.5–3 m) for discrete interval monitoring; longer for water supply
- Material: PVC (Schedule 40 or 80, most common for monitoring), stainless steel (316SS for contaminated sites), HDPE, PTFE (for trace organics)
- Diameter: 50 mm (2 in) standard for monitoring; 100–150 mm (4–6 in) for pump test or production wells

### Filter Pack (Sand Pack)
Clean, uniform silica sand placed in the annulus around the screen. Retains formation material while allowing free flow of water to the screen.

**Sizing:** Filter pack grain size = 4–6× the D50 of the formation material (for uniform formations). For well-graded formations, use 6–9× D30. Typical filter pack: ASTM C33 concrete sand or #2/12 silica sand (1–2 mm).

Filter pack extends from the bottom of the screen to 0.6–1.0 m (2–3 ft) above the top of the screen.

### Annular Seal
Prevents vertical migration of water along the borehole annulus. Critical for isolating the monitored zone.

**Materials:**
- Bentonite seal: 0.6–1.0 m (2–3 ft) of bentonite chips, pellets, or granular bentonite placed above the filter pack. Hydrates and swells to form a low-permeability seal.
- Grout: Cement-bentonite grout (3–5% bentonite by weight of water + neat cement) or bentonite grout tremied from the bentonite seal to the surface. Grout must be tremied from bottom-up to avoid bridging.

### Surface Completion
- **Flush-mount (traffic-rated):** Metal vault set flush with ground surface. Used in paved areas, traffic areas.
- **Stick-up (above-ground):** Protective steel casing (6–8 in diameter) with locking cap, set in concrete pad. Standard for non-traffic areas. Pad slopes away from well.
- **Concrete pad:** 0.6–1.0 m square, sloped for drainage, with protective bollards if in traffic area.

## Well Development

After installation, the well must be developed to remove drilling fluids, fine particles, and disturbed formation material from the filter pack and near-well zone. Restores natural hydraulic conductivity around the screen.

**Methods:**
- **Surging:** Plunger or surge block moved up and down in the casing to create alternating pressure pulses. Loosens fines.
- **Bailing:** Bailer lowered and raised to remove water and fines. Simple but slow.
- **Pumping:** Overpump the well (pump at higher rate than design), then reduce to design rate. Removes fines entrained by high-velocity flow.
- **Jetting:** High-pressure water directed at screen from inside. Aggressive — used for production wells.
- **Airlift:** Compressed air injected to lift water column. Effective but introduces air to formation (avoid in anaerobic zones or VOC-contaminated sites).

**Completion criteria:** Turbidity < 10 NTU, stabilized pH, temperature, and specific conductance. Minimum 3–5 casing volumes removed. Some specifications require < 5 NTU.

## Water Level Measurement

### Manual Methods
- **Electric water level indicator (e-tape):** Graduated tape with probe that completes a circuit on contact with water. Accuracy ±3 mm. Most common manual method.
- **Steel tape with chalk:** Chalked tape lowered into well; water line visible on chalk. Accuracy ±3 mm. Old-school but reliable.
- **Pressure transducer (manual read):** Lowered on cable; reads pressure head. Used for deep wells or artesian conditions.

### Automated Methods
- **Pressure transducers (dataloggers):** Vented or non-vented transducers continuously record water levels at programmable intervals (minutes to hours). Essential for pump tests, tidal analysis, seasonal monitoring.
- **Vented transducers:** Cable contains vent tube to atmosphere — reads gauge pressure directly. Preferred for most applications.
- **Non-vented (absolute) transducers:** Record absolute pressure — must correct for barometric pressure changes using a barometric logger.

### Reference Datum
All water level measurements referenced to a surveyed datum. Measure depth-to-water from a marked reference point (top of casing or reference mark on stick-up). Convert to elevation: WL elevation = reference point elevation - depth to water.

## Multi-Level Monitoring

When multiple aquifer zones or depth-discrete monitoring is needed:

- **Nested wells:** Multiple small-diameter wells in one borehole or adjacent boreholes, each screened at a different depth with bentonite seals between screens.
- **Westbay system:** Casing with multiple measurement ports isolated by packers. Allows sampling and pressure measurement at many discrete intervals in one borehole.
- **FLUTe systems:** Flexible liner used for multilevel monitoring in open boreholes (primarily rock).
- **Cluster wells:** Separate boreholes drilled close together, each completed at different depths. Most reliable isolation but most expensive.

## Decommissioning (Abandonment)

Wells no longer in use must be properly decommissioned to prevent cross-contamination between aquifers and surface water infiltration. Typically involves removing the pump/equipment, perforating or removing the casing, and filling the borehole with cement-bentonite grout from bottom to surface. Follow state/local regulations.
