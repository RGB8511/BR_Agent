# Standards Reference — Canal Structures

## USBR — Design of Small Canal Structures (1978)
**Scope:** The definitive USBR reference for irrigation canal structures. Covers check structures, drop structures (vertical, pipe, baffled apron), turnouts (pipe, open-channel, constant-head orifice), inverted siphons, road crossings, elevated flumes, wasteways, and transitions. Provides standard designs with drawings and sizing procedures. Out of print but widely available and still the standard of practice.

## USBR — Water Measurement Manual (3rd ed., 2001)
**Scope:** Comprehensive guide to flow measurement in open channels and pipelines. Covers weirs (sharp-crested, broad-crested, V-notch), flumes (Parshall, cutthroat, long-throated), orifices, current meters, acoustic methods, and data collection. Includes calibration equations, installation requirements, and accuracy assessments.

## USBR — Hydraulic Design of Stilling Basins and Energy Dissipators (EM-25)
**Scope:** Design of energy dissipation structures downstream of hydraulic structures. USBR Types I through VI stilling basins, impact basins, and baffled apron drops. Provides dimensionless design charts based on Froude number and unit discharge.

## FHWA HDS-5 — Hydraulic Design of Highway Culverts
**Scope:** Design of culverts under roads. Inlet and outlet control analysis, nomographs, performance curves. Directly applicable to pipe drops and canal road crossings where the canal passes through a culvert.

## USACE EM 1110-2-1603 — Hydraulic Design of Spillways
**Scope:** While primarily for dam spillways, contains relevant material on energy dissipation, stilling basin design, and chute flow that applies to canal chute drops and wasteway structures.

## USBR Small Water Structures (SWS) Series
**Scope:** Series of technical reports and standard designs for small water conveyance structures. Includes precast concrete structures, measurement devices, and standardized designs for common canal features. Enables efficient, economical construction of small structures.
