# Exploration Planning & Investigation Programs

## Investigation Phases

Geotechnical investigations follow a phased approach, each phase building on previous knowledge and reducing uncertainty progressively.

### Phase 1 — Desktop Study (Preliminary Assessment)
**Purpose:** Gather existing information before any fieldwork. Identify potential hazards, constraints, and data gaps.

**Sources:**
- Geologic maps (USGS, state geological surveys)
- Topographic maps and LiDAR/DEM data
- Aerial photographs (current and historical)
- Published soil surveys (NRCS Web Soil Survey)
- Previous geotechnical reports for the site and vicinity
- Groundwater well records (state databases)
- Seismic hazard maps
- Flood maps (FEMA)
- Environmental databases (EPA, state environmental agency)
- Historical land use (Sanborn maps, aerial photo archives)
- Mining records, karst databases

**Deliverable:** Desktop study report identifying geologic setting, anticipated subsurface conditions, potential hazards, and recommended scope for field investigation.

### Phase 2 — Site Reconnaissance
**Purpose:** Visual assessment of the site and surroundings.

**Activities:**
- Walk the site — observe topography, drainage, outcrops, seeps, springs
- Note evidence of landslides, settlement, erosion, sinkholes
- Identify access constraints (slopes, wetlands, overhead utilities, structures)
- Photograph key features
- Interview site owners/operators for site history
- Identify utility locations (call 811/One Call before any subsurface work)

### Phase 3 — Preliminary (Feasibility) Investigation
**Purpose:** Confirm general subsurface conditions; support feasibility-level design decisions; identify critical issues requiring further investigation.

**Typical scope:** Limited number of borings at key locations, standard testing (SPT, index properties), preliminary lab testing.

### Phase 4 — Detailed (Design-Level) Investigation
**Purpose:** Provide sufficient data for final design. Characterize all foundation conditions, determine design parameters, assess construction considerations.

**Scope:** Comprehensive boring/CPT program, detailed lab testing (strength, consolidation, permeability), in-situ testing (PMT, DMT as needed), groundwater monitoring, instrumentation.

### Phase 5 — Construction-Phase Investigation
**Purpose:** Verify conditions during construction; respond to changed conditions.

**Activities:** Proof drilling, foundation inspections, verification testing, instrumentation monitoring, contractor QA/QC.

## Boring Layout and Spacing

### General Principles
- Borings should characterize the full footprint of the proposed structure
- At least one boring at each significant foundation element
- Additional borings where conditions are expected to vary
- Depth must extend below the zone of influence of the structure

### Boring Depth Guidelines
- **Shallow foundations:** 2× the minimum foundation dimension below the foundation level, or until competent material is reached
- **Deep foundations (driven piles):** 6 m (20 ft) below anticipated pile tip, or 2× the pile group width in compressible soils
- **Deep foundations (drilled shafts):** 3–6 m (10–20 ft) below shaft tip, or 2× the shaft diameter in rock
- **Embankments/fills:** 1.5–2× the embankment height below original ground, or to firm stratum
- **Dams:** To depth where seepage and stability are not concerns; typically to sound rock (may be 30–100+ m)
- **Retaining walls:** 1.5× the wall height below the base, or to firm stratum
- **Terminate early only if:** continuous hard rock is encountered and verified for adequate depth

## Boring Logging

### Field Classification (ASTM D2488)
Visual-manual description of soil and rock in the field. Includes: soil type (USCS group), color, moisture condition, density/consistency, structure, and any inclusions or special features.

### Laboratory Classification (ASTM D2487)
Formal USCS classification based on grain size distribution and Atterberg limits testing. May differ from field classification — field log should be updated.

### Rock Core Logging
Description of: rock type, color, grain size, hardness, weathering, fracture frequency and condition (RQD, fracture spacing, aperture, infilling, roughness), core recovery (TCR, SCR, RQD), and discontinuity orientation if core is oriented.

### Boring Log Documentation
Complete boring log includes:
- Project information, boring number, location (coordinates), elevation
- Drilling method, bit size, casing information
- Driller name, logger name, date
- Soil/rock descriptions at each sample interval
- Sample types and depths (SPT, Shelby tube, core run)
- SPT blow counts (or CPT data references)
- Groundwater observations (during and after drilling)
- Drilling observations (loss of circulation, artesian conditions, caving, refusal)
- Laboratory test results (added after testing)

## Budget Considerations

Investigation cost is typically 0.5–2% of total project cost. Inadequate investigation is the leading cause of geotechnical failures and cost overruns. The value of additional borings almost always exceeds the cost of change orders, redesign, or failure.

**Rule of thumb:** It is far cheaper to drill one more boring than to redesign a foundation after construction begins.
