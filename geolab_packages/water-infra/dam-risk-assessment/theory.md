# Dam Risk Assessment & Potential Failure Modes

## Risk-Informed Decision Making (RIDM)

Modern dam safety has evolved from a purely standards-based approach (prescriptive factors of safety) to risk-informed decision making that considers the probability of failure, the consequences of failure, and the cost-effectiveness of risk reduction measures.

**Risk = Probability of Failure × Consequences of Failure**

RIDM does not replace traditional engineering analysis — it augments it by providing a framework to prioritize actions, allocate resources, and evaluate whether dam modifications are justified.

## Potential Failure Mode Analysis (PFMA)

PFMA is the cornerstone of risk assessment — systematically identifying how a dam could fail.

### Process
1. **Team assembly:** Dam safety engineers, geologists, hydrologists, operations staff, and facilitators
2. **Information review:** Design documents, construction records, instrumentation data, inspection reports, geological maps
3. **Failure mode identification:** Brainstorm all plausible ways the dam could fail
4. **Failure mode description:** Document each PFM as a complete chain: initiating condition → progression → detection/intervention → breach → consequences
5. **Screening and categorization:** Category I (highlighted — further analysis needed), Category II (considered but not highlighted), Category III (ruled out — physically not credible)
6. **Risk estimation:** For Category I PFMs, estimate probability and consequences

### Common Potential Failure Modes

**Embankment Dams:**
- Internal erosion through the embankment (piping through core, along conduits, through foundation)
- Internal erosion along the foundation contact
- Backward erosion piping in the foundation
- Overtopping (hydrologic or operational)
- Slope instability (upstream during rapid drawdown, downstream under steady-state or seismic)
- Liquefaction under seismic loading
- Spillway gate failure to open

**Concrete Dams:**
- Sliding on foundation (weak planes, uplift)
- Overturning (gravity dams)
- Foundation erosion from piping or seepage
- Abutment instability (arch dams)
- Alkali-silica reaction (ASR) — cracking, swelling, loss of strength
- Freeze-thaw deterioration
- Overtopping from inadequate spillway capacity

## Risk Estimation Methods

### Qualitative (PFMA Level)
Verbal descriptions of likelihood and consequence categories. Used for initial screening and prioritization.

### Semi-Quantitative Risk Analysis (SQRA)
Assigns numerical ranges to probability and consequence for each PFM:
- Annual probability of failure: orders of magnitude (10⁻², 10⁻³, etc.)
- Consequences: life loss estimates, economic damage
- Results plotted on f-N charts for comparison with tolerable risk guidelines

### Quantitative Risk Analysis (QRA)
Detailed probabilistic analysis with event trees:
- Hazard characterization (flood frequency, seismic hazard)
- System response (fragility curves, reliability analysis)
- Breach characteristics (outflow, timing)
- Consequence estimation (inundation modeling, life loss estimation)

## Tolerable Risk Guidelines

### USBR
- Individual risk: < 10⁻⁴ per year (risk to the most exposed individual)
- Societal risk: Annualized failure probability × life loss plotted on f-N chart
- ALARP (As Low As Reasonably Practicable) zone between tolerable and intolerable

### USACE
- Tolerable Risk Guidelines (TRG): Annual probability of failure < 10⁻⁴
- Life safety: Expected annual life loss should be reduced when Annualized Life Loss > 0.001 lives/year
- Uses a Tolerable Risk Matrix (probability vs. life loss)

### FERC
- Does not prescribe specific numerical risk targets
- Requires dam owners to identify, evaluate, and reduce risks from Category I PFMs
- Uses the SQRA framework for portfolio risk ranking

## Life Loss Estimation

Life loss depends on:
- **Population at risk (PAR):** People in the dam breach inundation zone
- **Warning time:** Time between breach initiation and flood arrival
- **Severity:** Flood depth, velocity, debris, time of day
- **Understanding:** Awareness that flooding is from a dam breach (affects evacuation response)

**Reclamation Consequence Estimating Methodology (RCEM):**
Fatality rate = f(flood severity, warning time, understanding)
- Flood severity: DV (depth × velocity) product and depth
- Warning time categories: < 15 min (no warning), 15–60 min, 1–12 hr, > 12 hr
- Fatality rates: 0.01 (low severity, long warning) to 1.0 (high severity, no warning)

## Internal Erosion — Key Risk Driver

Internal erosion is the leading cause of embankment dam failures worldwide:
- **Concentrated leak erosion:** Erosion along a pre-existing opening (crack, conduit, animal burrow)
- **Backward erosion piping (BEP):** Progressive erosion from the downstream toe upstream through foundation soils
- **Suffusion:** Selective erosion of fine particles from the matrix of an internally unstable soil
- **Contact erosion:** Erosion at the interface between coarse and fine layers under seepage flow

### Progression Assessment
For each internal erosion mode, assess:
1. Initiation: Does seepage gradient exceed critical gradient?
2. Continuation: Is there a filter or transition zone that would stop erosion?
3. Progression: Does the erosion pathway enlarge and connect through the dam?
4. Detection and intervention: Can the condition be detected and can reservoir be lowered in time?
5. Breach: If intervention fails, what is the breach mechanism and outflow?
