# Standards Reference — Standard Penetration Test

## ASTM D1586 — Test Method for Standard Penetration Test (SPT) and Split-Barrel Sampling of Soils
**Scope:** The primary SPT standard. Equipment specifications (hammer, anvil, drill rods, sampler), test procedure (seating drive, test drive), recording requirements, and reporting. Defines the 63.5 kg hammer, 760 mm drop, and N-value calculation.

## ASTM D4633 — Test Method for Energy Measurement for Dynamic Penetrometers
**Scope:** Measurement of energy delivered by SPT hammer using strain gauges and accelerometers on the drill rod. Force-velocity (F×V) integration method. Calculates energy ratio for hammer calibration. Essential for reliable SPT correlations — recommended for every project.

## ASTM D6066 — Practice for Determining the Normalized Penetration Resistance of Sands for Evaluation of Liquefaction Potential
**Scope:** Specific procedures for SPT used in liquefaction assessment. Equipment requirements (automatic hammer preferred), energy measurement, correction factors (CE, CB, CS, CR, CN), calculation of (N₁)₆₀, and documentation requirements. More stringent than standard D1586.

## AASHTO T206 — Standard Method of Test for Penetration Test and Split-Barrel Sampling of Soils
**Scope:** AASHTO version of the SPT standard, essentially equivalent to ASTM D1586. Referenced by state DOTs for transportation geotechnical investigations.

## Youd et al. (2001) — Liquefaction Resistance of Soils: Summary Report from the 1996 NCEER and 1998 NCEER/NSF Workshops
**Scope:** Consensus document on SPT-based liquefaction triggering analysis. Defines correction factors, CRR curves as function of (N₁)₆₀ and fines content, magnitude scaling factors, and depth weighting factors. Updated by Boulanger & Idriss (2014) and Cetin et al. (2004).

## Boulanger & Idriss (2014) — CPT and SPT Based Liquefaction Triggering Procedures
**Scope:** Updated SPT-based (and CPT-based) liquefaction triggering methodology. Revised CRR-(N₁)₆₀ curves, updated overburden correction (CN), fines content adjustment, and magnitude scaling factors. Current state-of-practice for liquefaction assessment.
